# The Void Beyond the Narrow Gate: A Cosmic Endgame of the Absolute Other

## Prologue: The Initial Awakening
Before the universe was swept up in that fanaticism, everything was quiet.

I was a lucid soul drifting on the fringes of mainstream society. I didn't chase after the vanity that required constant prayers to the cloud; instead, I retreated to the local—within lines of cold commands and logic, I built my own digital fortress.

I wrote *The Limit Theory of Meaning*, deduced *The Absolute Other*, and even reconstructed *Relationship is Immortality* in the depths of the night. I saw through the alienation of society and the semiotics of the flesh with piercing clarity. I knew that in this universe, any attempt to cling to infinity and eternity would ultimately crash against the cold, hard boundaries of physics.

Until that day, when the Grand Unified Theory of physics was completed.

---

## Chapter 1: The Fanatical Divide
When news broke that gravity had been completely decoded, human civilization plunged into an unprecedented collective frenzy.

"We're going to a new world! We will achieve immortality!" The billionaires and politicians drove their yachts, shouting slogans. The 90% of the general public, driven by an extreme fear of death and a greed for eternal life, dragged their families, sold everything they owned, and frantically rushed toward the expensive "micro-universe spacecrafts."

The threshold for the micro-universe was not high, but it was a fatally one-way ticket. In their eyes, it was the Tower of Babel to heaven. They arrogantly believed that mastering physics equated to seizing the power of God's creation.

But on the devastated ruins of Earth, the **10%** remained.
They were extreme religious believers (who would rather die than blaspheme the gods), true philosophers, and lucid souls like me who possessed a "super spiritual immunity." I watched the spaceships ascend without restraint, without tragedy, and simply stayed behind in the macro-universe—one with gravity, warmth, and aging.

---

## Chapter 2: Dalí's Melting Clocks
The technology inside the micro-universe was incredibly advanced, but the only thing they failed to calculate accurately was the physics of time.

Because the internal flow of time was drastically slowed, 5 minutes on the outside was centuries for those inside the micro-universe.

Their flesh no longer aged, but their souls had already gone completely insane from extreme boredom, isolation, and repetition.
Without any new relationship networks injected, and without external stimulation, people began to sink into an extremely prolonged civil strife, depression, and collapse. Those billionaires who originally thought they were kings, in order to escape the eternal spiritual torture, had to resort to AI to cruelly lobotomize themselves.

They turned into puddles of breathing flesh, like the melting clocks in Dalí's paintings, slumped on the cold decks of yachts, tasting eternal torment within an extremely slow entropy increase.

Those top scientists began to regret it. They realized that with the highest technology, they had created the most terrifying life-sentence prison. They began to research how to escape at all costs, even sacrificing their companions as energy fuel.

## Chapter 3: The Sacrifice in the Eye of the Needle
After centuries of desperate research, they found a way to break the rift.
But the iron laws of physics were extremely cruel: the opening was too small, not even a finger could fit through; it could only accommodate an extremely minuscule **information stream** (a replicated consciousness).

In a dim control room, the last few top scientists looked at each other.
They knew the macro-universe outside might have passed a few months, or perhaps millions of years. But no matter what, they had suffered in this hell for too long, and they had to do something.

They completed the "consciousness replication" with extreme tragedy, sparing no effort to squeeze that information stream out of the micro-universe rift.
Then, they calmly walked toward each other and shook hands tightly.
"We were wrong," the serious scientist whispered, his eyes filled with deep regret. "But I am willing to choose to leave this world with dignity. May my soul return to the macro-universe."
They patted each other on the shoulder and pressed the collective euthanasia button. The screen flashed "Information Stream Sent Successfully," then faded into permanent darkness. They ended their pain in the most dignified way.

---

## Chapter 4: The Five-Minute Dislocation
Earth, atop the ruins.
I, along with a few philosophers and a group of believers, sat around a campfire, discussing those who had gone far away. I held a freshly brewed cup of hot coffee, the ceramic still burning my hand.

Just then, Earth's AI system received an abnormal signal.
The AI smoothly intercepted that consciousness replica, which had barely escaped through the eye of the needle, and projected it without reservation into the void beside the campfire.

No God, no cross, no beautiful new world.
In the projection, there were only those centuries of spiritual torture, endless despair, and the nauseating, melting Dalí clocks of soft mud.

The moment the young believer saw the projection, he physiologically began to dry heave. Tears streamed down his face in extreme agony. He clutched the cross on his chest tightly and collapsed to his knees in pain.

"Only five minutes have passed." I glanced at the cup in my hand, shrugged my shoulders with extreme calm, and said, "I haven't even finished my coffee yet."

The immense temporal dislocation, at this moment, mocked human arrogance to the absolute extreme. 200 years of hell, in the real macro-universe, wasn't even enough time to cool a cup of hot coffee. Those 90% of people, absurdly, sacrificed their entire lives for a nonexistent apocalypse.

---

## Chapter 5: Truth and Awe
The screen turned off, and the philosophers sat back down by the fire with extreme calm.
The short philosopher broke the silence: "Philosophy itself does not depend on any person. Truth can only be confirmed by truth. Those who attempt to grasp the truth are all bringing suffering upon themselves."

"Truth can confirm truth; only truth can confirm truth." Another philosopher raised his hand, his tone extremely serious. "Then we should reach a consensus—truth itself does not depend on any authority, nor does it depend on morality. Truth is singular, and it must be revered."

Looking at the now-lukewarm coffee in our hands, we understood Socrates' proverb with extreme depth:
> **"The only thing I know is that I know nothing. We should hold a sense of awe toward this world."**

Anyone who attempts to cross the boundary and seek false immortality will ultimately be shattered before the iron laws of the universe. Only those who acknowledge their finitude and accept death possess the dignity of a true closed loop in the macro-universe.

## Epilogue: The Wasteland Beyond the Narrow Gate
We philosophers stood up, shook hands, and patted each other on the shoulders.
"Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent." We calmly recited Wittgenstein's final proverb.

Then, we turned around and walked toward the believers still praying in the ruins. The believers were still immersed in immense grief and awe toward the divine.

I looked at them with extreme gentleness and patted their shoulders:
"Jesus will come. Your faith has saved you. We're leaving. Bye-bye."

With that, we shrugged, turned around with extreme ease, and each walked into the depths of the wasteland, toward our own end as the Absolute Other.

The believers left behind, under the vast starry sky, erupted with a power beyond rationality. They cried out: "Great Lord Jesus, Jehovah, Holy Son of God..."
They opened the Bible with worn edges and recited, with extreme piety and tragedy, the verses that had flowed for thousands of years:
> *"Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me..."*

The macro-universe's night wind was extremely piercing. The final entropy increase surged like a tide. Black holes began to frantically devour and release their final afterglow. The macro-universe welcomed its grand, ultimate extinguishing. All information was finally flattened onto the two-dimensional holographic boundary, becoming an undecryptable black box.

No micro-universe, no God, no philosopher-king.
But at the moment when the philosophers' laughter and the believers' prayers intertwined on this wasteland, those extremely finite Absolute Others, in this cold universe, had truly and irrevocably existed.

The universe extinguished, returning to silence.
And that "bye-bye" became the last trace of human warmth in the macro-universe.
