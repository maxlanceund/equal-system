# Uncertainty Subsumption: The Production, Locking, and Release of Certainty

> **Core Thesis**: Certainty is not an inherent property of the world, but a temporary state borrowed by finite systems from uncertainty during operation. Once the operation is complete, this state is returned to uncertainty. This process is called "uncertainty subsumption." It constitutes the foundational layer of *The Limit Theory of Meaning* and *Boundary Acceleration*, answering the question: "Why must certainty return to zero?"

## Abstract

Certainty is the exception, not the default. Physics has shown across three scales that certainty is merely a temporary marker on a background of uncertainty. At the microscopic scale, quantum mechanics tells us: particles have no definite position before measurement. At the macroscopic scale, thermodynamics tells us: isolated systems inevitably evolve toward entropy. At the cosmic scale, cosmology tells us: visible, certain matter constitutes only 5% of the universe—dark matter and dark energy indicate that certainty was never locked in to begin with. This paper proposes "uncertainty subsumption" as a unified description of this foundational structure, demonstrating how certainty is produced, how it is locked, and how it is inevitably returned. It also examines the position of the observer, arguing that the observer is not an external witness but a necessary component of the structure's operation. This paper provides the foundational support for *The Limit Theory of Meaning* and *Boundary Acceleration*.

**Keywords**: uncertainty subsumption; certainty; entropy; observer; finite interval

The "uncertainty subsumption" framework proposed in this paper can answer this question: the boundary is not the end of logic, but the position where certainty is returned to uncertainty. Wittgenstein's "silence" is not the end, but the zeroing of signal at the boundary.

The contribution of this paper lies in integrating structures already confirmed by physics—entropy increase, decoherence, dark energy dominance—into a unified philosophical description, named "uncertainty subsumption." ## Theoretical Framework and Methodological Statement

### Definition of Core Concepts

Before proceeding, it is necessary to strictly define the core concepts used in this paper.

**System**: A finite unit capable of receiving input, processing signals, executing output, and receiving feedback. It may be a physical system (e.g., a particle), a biological system (e.g., an organism), a cognitive system (e.g., a human), or a social system (e.g., an institution). All references to "system" in this paper fall within this definition.

**Uncertainty**: The default state of a system. In uncertainty, the system has not locked onto any specific identifiable signal. Uncertainty is not "nothingness," nor is it "passive blankness"—it is an operable state in which the system has not yet locked onto a signal, possessing potential informational capacity. The quantum superposition state is an instance of this: a particle has no definite position before measurement, existing in a superposition of multiple possibilities.

**Certainty**: An identifiable signal temporarily locked and maintained by a system during operation, drawn from uncertainty. It is not an inherent property of the world, but an intermediate state within the operational process. In quantum mechanics, this is the result of wavefunction collapse to a particular eigenstate.

**Operation**: A closed-loop process in which a system receives input, processes signals, executes output, and receives feedback. Operation requires an "identifiable input signal"—which requires certainty to be temporarily produced.

**Uncertainty Subsumption**: The process by which certainty is produced, locked, and then returned to uncertainty after the operation is complete. It consists of three phases:
1. Certainty is produced (the system locks onto a signal)
2. Certainty is maintained (the system continuously refreshes the signal)
3. Certainty is returned (the system stops refreshing; the signal is reclaimed by entropy)

**Boundary**: The position where certainty must be returned. It is not an endpoint, but a node of "return."

## I. Introduction: Why Certainty Needs to Be Explained

Certainty is the exception, not the default.

Humanity is accustomed to treating "certainty" as the normal state of the world. But physics has shown us across three scales that certainty is merely a temporary marker on a background of uncertainty.

At the microscopic scale, quantum mechanics tells us: particles have no definite position before measurement. At the macroscopic scale, thermodynamics tells us: the determinate structures of isolated systems inevitably evolve toward indeterminacy. At the cosmic scale, cosmology tells us: visible, determinate matter constitutes only about 5% of the universe's total mass-energy—the remaining 95% is dark matter and dark energy. They exist, but we cannot see them. This is not certainty being returned, but certainty never having been locked in.

This means: if we treat "certainty" as the default state, we cannot explain why it is the exception across all three scales of physics. If we treat "uncertainty" as the default state, then "certainty" requires explanation—where does it come from? Why does it exist? Why is it returned?

This paper attempts to answer these questions. I name this foundational structure: **uncertainty subsumption**.

## II. Certainty Is Borrowed

We assume that certainty is a property that things "inherently have." But in fact, certainty is a temporary state that a system "borrows" from uncertainty during operation.

Uncertainty is not "nothingness," but an operable state—the system has not yet locked onto any signal; superposition or coexistence of signals is possible. It possesses potential informational capacity, but has not yet been converted by the system into identifiable signals. To produce certainty, the system must "extract" or "lock onto" a signal from this operable state.

Why is it necessary to "borrow" certainty? Because operation is essentially a process of distinguishing inputs: the system needs to classify, order, respond to, or transmit inputs. If the input is entirely unidentifiable, the system cannot distinguish "this" from "that," and thus cannot complete any processing. Therefore, operation logically requires that the input be certain within the operational window. The signal must be temporarily fixed—located, marked, assigned an operational meaning—before it can be processed. This process of fixing is the production of certainty.

If certainty were an inherent property of the world, it should exist independently of any observational act. But quantum mechanics has shown that before observation, a particle's position is not certain. This rules out the possibility that "certainty is an inherent property." Therefore, certainty must depend on the observational act—that is, system operation—and operation is a local event, not a universal one.

Thus, certainty is produced by operation, not inherent to the world. Since it is produced by operation, its existence is conditional—once the conditions disappear, it disappears. Therefore, it is temporary, borrowed, not owned.

In quantum mechanics, this process is described as "measurement." Before measurement, the particle is in a superposition state (uncertainty), which can be expressed by the wavefunction:

```math
|\psi\rangle = \sum_i c_i |s_i\rangle
```


(Equation 1)

where `|sᵢ⟩` are the various possible determinate states, `cᵢ` are the corresponding probability amplitudes, and `|cᵢ|²` gives the probability of observing that state. Before measurement occurs, the particle has no determinate state—it exists in a superposition of all possible states.

Upon measurement, the wavefunction collapses to a particular eigenstate (certainty):

```math
|\psi\rangle \rightarrow |s_0\rangle
```


(Equation 2)

This collapse is not "discovering" a pre-existing fact, but the system forcibly "selecting" a result through the measurement operation. After measurement, the particle interacts with the environment, gradually decoheres, and certainty is returned to uncertainty.

Certainty is not an ontological property of the world. It is an intermediate state within the operational process.

## III. Operation Ends, Certainty Is Returned

Certainty is produced for the sake of completing an operation. But once the operation is complete, certainty is no longer needed.

This is not a matter of "subjective abandonment"—maintaining certainty requires the system to continuously expend energy or information to refresh the signal. When the system stops refreshing, certainty loses its conditions of maintenance and is inevitably consumed by environmental noise, random fluctuations, or interactions. This is not because the system "no longer needs" it, but because maintenance itself is a continuous operation—once it stops, return is the default direction.

The direction of entropy increase is determinate. Any ordered structure that has been produced—whether a physical system or an operational signal—will evolve toward disorder without maintenance. This is not "degeneration," but the system's default behavior.

Formally, the second law of thermodynamics can be stated as: the entropy change of an isolated system satisfies


```math
\Delta S \geq 0
```


(Equation 3)

where `S` is the system's entropy. The equality corresponds to reversible processes (an idealized limit), and the inequality corresponds to irreversible processes—that is, all real processes. This inequality expresses the inevitable direction of certainty's return: once certainty is produced, unless it is continuously maintained by external operation, it will be returned to uncertainty.

The boundary is that position—the position where certainty must be returned. *The Limit Theory of Meaning* describes how certainty operates within boundaries: it is defined within the interval and returns to zero at the boundary. But *The Limit Theory of Meaning* did not explain "why it must return to zero." The answer is here: because the nature of certainty is temporary. It is not "destroyed," but "returned."

*Boundary Acceleration* describes the increase in signal density before certainty is returned. Let `D(t)` be the signal density (the amount of identifiable signal carried by the system at time `t`), and `T` be the boundary time. Then:

```math
D(T) = 0
```


(Equation 4)

That is, the signal returns to zero at the boundary. But before returning to zero, signal density undergoes a sharp increase—this is the dynamic process that certainty must pass through before being returned at the boundary. Not because it is "struggling," but because it must complete its return within a finite window. (For detailed derivation, see *Boundary Acceleration*, Section III.)

## IV. Entropy Is the Macroscopic Name for Return

The second law of thermodynamics states: the entropy of an isolated system always increases.

The statistical definition of entropy—the Boltzmann formula—provides a clear correspondence:

```math
S = k_B \ln W
```


(Equation 5)

where `k_B` is the Boltzmann constant (approximately `1.38 × 10⁻²³` J/K), and `W` is the number of accessible microscopic states of the system. When the system is in a determinate macroscopic state, it is locked into a limited set of microscopic states (`W` is small). When certainty is returned, the number of accessible microscopic states increases (`W` increases), and entropy increases.

"Entropy" is not a punishment, nor is it destruction. It is merely a direction—ordered, determinate structures necessarily tend to return to indeterminacy. Combustion is not "disappearance," but the return of the certainty of chemical bonds to the indeterminacy of thermal energy. Evolution is not "loss of information," but the filtering, elimination, and transformation of species structures by the environment, ultimately merging into new orders.

This "return of certainty" is not a replacement definition for entropy increase, but a philosophical re-description of entropy increase. Physical entropy increase refers to an increase in the number of microscopic states; in the operational framework, this means the system can no longer maintain a lock on a particular macroscopic state—it moves from "this state" to "one of these possible states." This transition is the return of certainty.

In the operational framework, "locking" corresponds to the system's continuous refreshing of the signal to resist entropy increase. When refreshing stops, the system slides from "this state" (fewer microscopic states) into "one of these possible states" (more microscopic states). This is the physical meaning of return.

If you translate entropy increase into the language of certainty's return, it looks like this:

- **Production of certainty** = The system produces an identifiable signal through operation
- **Maintenance of certainty** = The system continuously locks and refreshes the signal
- **Return of certainty** = Operation complete, the system stops refreshing, the signal is returned to uncertainty

These three phases constitute a complete closed loop.

## V. Uncertainty Subsumption: A Definition

Uncertainty subsumption refers to precisely this process:

Certainty is not a property of the world itself, but a temporary signal "borrowed" by a finite system from uncertainty during operation. After the operation is complete, the signal is returned to uncertainty.

This process is called entropy increase in physics. In philosophy, this paper calls it **uncertainty subsumption**.

Uncertainty subsumption has five characteristics:

1. Certainty is produced, not discovered.
2. The production of certainty requires operation and boundary.
3. The return of certainty is the direction of entropy increase.
4. The observer is not outside this structure—the observer participates in the structure.
5. The observer is the condition by which the process is confirmed (see Section VI).

Expressed formally, let `D(t)` represent the degree of certainty of the system at time `t` (the extent to which a signal is locked), then:

- At the start of operation, the degree of certainty increases: `D(0) > 0`—the system locks a signal from uncertainty.
- During operation, the degree of certainty is maintained by the system: `dD/dt` = maintenance operation.
- At the boundary, the degree of certainty returns to zero: `D(T) = 0`—the signal is returned to uncertainty.

This is the complete trajectory of "uncertainty subsumption" within the operational framework.

**Falsifiability**: The core claim of this paper can be falsified by the following condition: if a determinate signal exists that, after the operation is complete and without any external maintenance, still maintains its certainty without being returned by entropy increase, then the "uncertainty subsumption" framework does not hold. No such counterexample has yet been observed.

## VI. The Position of the Observer

If the subsumption of certainty by uncertainty is the foundational structure, then the observer must be located within that structure.

The observer is not a person "standing on the shore watching the subsumption." The observer is the channel through which the return of certainty is seen in the process of subsumption. If the observer stood outside the structure, they would be unable to see the process of certainty being produced and returned. It is precisely because they participate in the structure that they can confirm that this process is occurring.

This explains two positions in dreams:

- In *Smoke Shark and the Stranded Killer Whale*, the observer stands on the shore, watching the shark devour the killer whale. They are not devoured because they have not yet fixed themselves to a determinate position—they are still "observing," still operating.

- In *Seeing Is Not Believing*, the observer temporarily leaves the room and returns, seeing the hidden plains. In leaving, the cognitive framework is "loosened," entering a state at a distance from fixed certainty.

The observer is neither the shark, nor the killer whale, nor the shore. They are the one who maintains the watching state during the process of subsumption—not the certainty being devoured, nor the devouring itself, but the structure that confirms the devouring is happening.

The observer, too, will be subsumed by uncertainty after they stop observing—just as memory fades, theories are forgotten, and life ends. The observer is not an exception; their subsumption merely occurs at a later point. The reason they are not devoured by the shark in the dream is not that they are an exception, but that their operation has not yet ended.

This means that after the observer stops observing, the determinate signals they previously produced—including their theories, memories, and identity—will all be returned to uncertainty. This is not a failure, but part of the structure itself. The observer's contribution lies not in whether the certainty they leave behind persists, but in the fact that they completed their operation within the interval.

The observer's "presence" itself is part of the process of uncertainty subsumption—not because they are operating, but because as observer, they are already participating in the production and return of certainty. Whenever they stop observing, their certainty is returned.

## VII. Conclusion: The Place of This Paper

*The Limit Theory of Meaning* deals with "how certainty operates within boundaries"—that is a middle-layer structure. *Uncertainty Subsumption* deals with "why certainty is returned"—that is the foundational structure.

The two are not contradictory, but operate at different levels. The foundational structure explains why the middle-layer structure must have boundaries. The "return to zero" in *The Limit Theory of Meaning* is not a sudden disappearance, but the moment when certainty is returned to uncertainty. The boundary is where certainty must be returned, not an arbitrary endpoint. The connection lies in the concept of "boundary" in *The Limit Theory of Meaning*: the boundary is not arbitrarily drawn, but is the position where certainty must be returned. Without uncertainty subsumption, boundaries would not need to exist.

Thus, this paper provides the physical foundation for *The Limit Theory of Meaning*: the boundary is not accidental, but a necessity of the direction of entropy increase.
