# Relation Is Immortality: From the Emptiness of the Absolute Other to Relational Continuity

## Abstract

This paper is the sequel to *The Absolute Other: An Ontological Critique from Genetic Randomness to the Objectification of Consciousness* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026). The earlier work dismantled the “I” into the absolute other, leaving behind an “un-kidnapped state”—free, but empty. This paper responds to that emptiness. It does not do so through twenty-nine philosophers, cosmological extrapolation, or a critique of the entire substance-ontology tradition. It does so through one thesis: **the individual is not an isolated substance but a node in the relational network; death terminates the individual’s operational interval, but does not erase its relational traces.**

In this paper, “immortality” refers specifically to **relational immortality**: relation does not vanish when an individual’s operational interval terminates. **“Immortality” does not mean the continuation of individual consciousness, personal identity, or the soul; immortality is the immortality of relation, not of the individual.**

The core argument is Section II.5: **relation precedes the entity**. This is not “relation precedes existence itself” (this paper does not discuss the thing-in-itself), but rather: any entity, in order to be identified and determined, must already be in relation. This argument does not rely on the authority of any philosopher; it relies only on the usage of the word “entity.”

This paper enters into limited dialogue with Marx, Whitehead, and Buddhist dependent origination, because they directly address “relation and continuity.” It does not address Aristotle, Descartes, or Kant—their substance ontology is not this paper’s opponent. This paper’s opponent is the emptiness left by the earlier work. It provides everyday evidence, extreme marginal cases, a practical dimension, and falsification conditions. **This paper does not claim personal immortality, nor does it deny the reality of death. It claims: relational continuity is structural; death takes away the individual, but not all relation.**

**Keywords**: relational continuity; absolute other; transcendental covenant; objective immortality; relation precedes the entity

## 0. Terminology

The following core terms are used with strict definitions. After their first occurrence, these definitions govern all subsequent uses.

**Immortality (relational immortality)**: In this paper, “immortality” refers specifically to relational immortality—relation does not vanish when an individual’s operational interval terminates. **“Immortality” does not mean the continuation of individual consciousness, personal identity, or the soul.** The slogan “you have never died” uses “you” in the sense of a relational node, not a personal ego. Biological death really occurs.

**Relation precedes the entity**: “Precedes” here means constitutive priority, not temporal priority. Relation is not a new first substance; it is the constitutive condition of entities. Any entity, in order to be identified and determined, must already be in relation. This paper does not discuss “existence itself” or the thing-in-itself.

**Absolute other**: The constitutive conditions that an individual cannot autonomously choose. This paper does not claim “the absolute other is something outside relation”; it claims “the absolute other operates in the form of relation.”

**Transcendental covenant**: Not a transaction, but the relational network’s prior signing-for of the individual. You cannot refuse to sign, because refusal itself requires the support of the relational network.

**Projection**: The temporary presentation of the relational network on a particular carrier. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. The projection dissolves; relation does not.

**Objective immortality**: In Whitehead’s sense—traces become material for subsequent occasions. **Objective immortality ≠ personal immortality.**

**Relational node**: The position and function of an individual in the relational network. After the carrier terminates, the individual becomes a historical node / objective material.

**Operational interval**: The interval of experience in which a finite system produces meaning. Death is the termination of the operational interval.

# I. Introduction: Starting from an Intuition in *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*

When I read *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*, I felt something. Old Namiya dies, but his letters still affect the three thieves. Katsuro dies, but his “Rebirth” is still sung by Seri and heard by Harumi. The author will die, but the book remains in the reader’s hands.

I followed this thing. It is not a “warm story”; it is harder than warmth. It is a structure: **people die, but relation remains.**

This intuition led me to a question: if the “I” is not a substance but relation, what exactly does death take away?

*The Absolute Other* answered the first half: death takes away the absolute other labeled “I.” But it left an emptiness. It said the “un-kidnapped state” is freedom, but freedom is not the same as non-emptiness. This paper responds to that emptiness: what remains after the “I” is dismantled is not nothingness, but relation.

This paper does only this one thing. It does not dialogue with twenty-nine philosophers, does not address the substance-ontology tradition, and does not extrapolate to cosmology. Its opponent is only one: the emptiness left by the earlier work.

---

# II. Relation Precedes the Subject: Completing *The Absolute Other*

In *The Absolute Other*, I argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other. That which is labeled “I” comes entirely from outside; it is entirely relation.

But that conclusion was cold. It brought deconstruction, stripping, and the absolute freedom of “the other is dead, the I is born”—but also emptiness.

Now I turn this conclusion to its other side: **if the “I” is the absolute other, then the “I” is part of relation.**

**A key argument must be supplied: why is the “absolute other” relation?**

The earlier work argued that the “I” is the absolute other, but “absolute other” there meant **all externality**—genes, language, society, death. These externalities are not identical with relation. This paper must argue why the absolute other is relation:

- **Genes are relation**: a gene is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between parent and offspring.
- **Language is relation**: language is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between signifier and signified, speaker and listener.
- **Society is relation**: society is not an isolated “thing”; it is relations among people.
- **Death is relation**: death is not an isolated “thing”; it is the termination of an operational interval, the point at which relation shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.”

Therefore, the absolute other is relation. It is not something “outside relation”; it is relation itself.

You are troubled because you think you are an independent “I,” and the “absolute other” threatens this “I.” But if you were never that “I,” and are instead relation itself, then what is there to be troubled about?

Thus *Relation Is Immortality* is not a negation of *The Absolute Other* but its **completion**. You first deconstruct the “I” completely; then you discover that what remains after deconstruction is not nothingness but the **relational network**.

**The earlier work dismantled the “I”; this paper dismantles “loneliness.” The earlier work said, “You have nothing”; this paper says, “You have never lost anything.” But this paper’s warmth is not consolation; it is a structural statement: you are not immortal as a substance; you are immortal as a relational node.**

## II.5 Relation Precedes the Entity

The preceding sections argued that “the absolute other operates in the form of relation.” But this is not yet enough. Someone may say: even if the “I” is constituted by relation, that only shows that the “I” as an entity depends on relation; it does not show that “relation precedes the entity.” Relation might be a property of entities rather than their precondition.

This section gives a more precise argument. It does not concern “existence itself” (which this paper does not discuss); it concerns only the **entity**—that is, a concrete thing that can be identified and determined.

### Definitions

**Entity**: Something that can be identified and determined.

**Relation**: The structure of difference between two terms. The distinction between A and B is relation.

**Constitutive priority**: X precedes Y if and only if, for Y to be determined, X must already be presupposed.

### Argument

The word “entity” presupposes “identifiability.” A “thing” that cannot be identified by anything cannot be subsumed under the category “entity”—not because it does not exist, but because it is conceptually indistinguishable from “nothing.”

To identify something is to distinguish it from other things. Distinction is relation. Therefore, for something to be subsumed under the category “entity,” it must already be in relation.

This does not mean “relation is temporally prior to entities.” It means: relation is the constitutive condition for the concept “entity” to hold. Without relation, there is no category “entity.”

### The Limits of This Argument

This argument does not concern “existence itself.” It does not claim that “relation creates existence,” nor that “existence itself depends on relation.” It claims only: the concept “entity” presupposes relation.

In other words: this paper does not discuss whether “things-in-themselves” exist. It discusses only what we mean when we say “something exists.” We mean: something can be identified and determined. And “can be identified and determined” presupposes relation.

### Objections and Responses

**Objection 1: What if there is a “thing-in-itself” that exists but is never identified by anything?**

**Response**: This paper does not deny that such a “thing-in-itself” may exist. It points out only that it cannot be subsumed under the category “entity.” You may say it “exists,” but your “exists” has no determination; it equals “nothing.” This is not this paper’s arbitrary stipulation; it is the usage of the word “entity.”

**Objection 2: This argument only proves “relation is the condition of identification,” not “relation is the condition of existence.”**

**Response**: Correct. This paper claims only “relation is the constitutive condition of the entity,” not “relation is the condition of existence.” The former is conceptual analysis; the latter is an ontological assertion. This paper does not make the latter.

**Objection 3: This argument is circular.**

**Response**: This is not circular; it is conceptual analysis. The meaning of “entity” includes “identifiability.” This paper reveals this usage; it does not smuggle it in as a premise.

**Objection 4: If relation precedes the entity, is “relation” itself an entity?**

**Response**: This paper does not claim “relation is an entity.” Relation is not a “thing”; relation is the constitutive condition of entities. Asking “is relation an entity” is like asking “is red a color”—it confuses levels.

### The Place of This Argument in the Paper

This argument is the core of Section II. It completes what the preceding sections did not: the preceding sections argued that “the absolute other operates in the form of relation”; this argument proves that “relation is the constitutive condition of the entity.”

But the limits of this argument must be noted: it does not prove “relation precedes existence”; it proves only “relation precedes the entity.” The title’s “relation precedes all” is shorthand for “relation precedes the entity.” As for “existence itself,” this paper does not discuss it.

With this argument, Sections III (transcendental covenant), IV (death and objective immortality), and V (projection and relation) acquire a hard foundation.

# III. The Transcendental Covenant: You Are Signed For at Birth

Christianity says that the relation between human beings and God is a covenant. I used to find this laughable, because it seemed like a transaction: you believe in him, he blesses you.

But now I find it somewhat reasonable. For if you push “relation” to the ontological bottom, then “covenant” is no longer a legal metaphor but an **ontological description**.

In Christian theology, the core of “covenant” is not “I exchange conditions with you,” but **“I make a covenant with you, and bring you into my relation.”** God covenants with Abraham not because Abraham performed well, but because God actively draws Abraham into a relational network. This network precedes Abraham’s merit, precedes his descendants, even precedes his existence.

This is almost the same structure as “as long as you are born, you are already established.” The only difference is that Christianity names the source of this relational network “God,” while I name it “the sum of all relations.”

I call this the **transcendental covenant**. It is not a condition you negotiate with some God; it is the relational network’s “receipt” for you at birth. You have no signing right, because the act of signing is already part of relation. The only thing you can do is not to refuse the covenant, but to **acknowledge that it has already happened**.

You cannot refuse to sign, because “refusal” itself requires the support of the relational network—if you want to live in seclusion, you still need land, water, and air.

# IV. Death Is Not the Breaking of Relation: From Direct Participation to Objective Immortality

In *The Limits of Meaning*, I argued that meaning is a signal produced by a finite system through closed-loop operation within a finite interval of experience; outside the interval, meaning is undefined—not zeroed, but no longer defined.

Death is the termination of the interval of experience. After death, the meaning of the “I” is likewise undefined.

But this is not the endpoint of this paper. This paper advances further: **what death takes away is the absolute other labeled “I,” not relation itself.**

You do not live as a “thing”; you live as a **relational node**. The node may vanish biologically, but the relations it carries—the words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—still operate in the relational network.

Whitehead has a concept called **“objective immortality.”** Every actual occasion, after it occurs, becomes “objective material” for subsequent occasions. Your life is a process; when it ends, you do not disappear—you become a **condition of subsequent processes**.

**A crucial clarification: “objective immortality” is not “immortality.”**

“Objective immortality” says, “Your traces remain.” “Immortality” says, “Relation has not vanished.” There is a conceptual slide between these two propositions.

- **Objective immortality**: your traces remain.
- **Immortality (relational immortality)**: relation itself has not vanished.

The difference is that traces are one layer of relation, not the whole of relation. You are “immortal” not because your traces remain, but because **you have always been relation itself, and relation is not a trace**.

# V. Projection and Relation: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation

This paper advances a thesis more radical than “death is not the breaking of relation”: **the “you” is not an independent substance at all, but only a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network.**

**Step One: You are not the master of relation; you are a node of relation.**

Since the “I” is wholly constituted by relation, the “I” is not the master of relation but a **node** of relation. A node is not an independent entity; it is an intersection of the relational network. You think “you” are living; in fact the relational network is operating through this node.

**Step Two: The node is a temporary projection of the relational network.**

Use the metaphor of light: sunlight falls on water and forms a gleam. The gleam is not the water; it is only the reflection of sunlight on the water’s surface. You see the gleam and think it is an independent thing, but it is only a **temporary projection** of water, sunlight, and air. The sun leaves; the gleam vanishes; but the water remains, and the air remains.

“You” are like this. You are not an independent substance; you are a temporary projection of genes, language, society, history, and death.

**Step Three: The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network.**

Those who died tens of thousands of years ago—their projections vanished, but the information never vanished. For information was never in the projection; it was always in relation. Their genes are in your body; their language is in your mouth; their tools are in your hands. These are not “legacies they left behind”; they are **they themselves**.

**Step Four: The projection vanishes; relation remains.**

Therefore, death is not the vanishing of “you,” but the vanishing of the “projection.” When the biological body dies, this mode of presentation terminates, but the relational network does not thereby break.

Jesus died, but his relation remains. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” remains. **They do not live as “souls”; they live as relational nodes.**

**Conclusion: You are not you; you are the temporary projection of relation. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. Death takes away only the projection; relation itself has never vanished.**

---

**Supplementary Section: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation**

Someone may say: you argue that relation does not vanish with death, but then you jump from “relation does not vanish” to “you have not died.” There is a conceptual slide. “Relation remains” does not equal “you remain.”

The answer is: **the word “you” never referred to the projection.** The projection is only a temporary mode of presentation; it is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. You mistakenly think “you” are that projection, and so you fear death. But once you see through this, you discover: the “you” that would die was never you. You are relation; relation is immortal.

This clarification has three steps:

**(a) The projection vanishes.** The biological carrier stops; the projection vanishes. This is a structural fact.

**(b) Relation does not vanish.** The words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined still operate in the relational network. This too is a structural fact.

**(c) You are not the projection; you are relation.** This is the core thesis. You are not the projection that vanishes; you are the relation that does not vanish.

Metaphor: a wave is a temporary form of seawater. The wave vanishes, but the seawater does not. You are not the wave; you are the seawater.

**Conclusion: You are not the projection; you are relation. The projection vanishes; relation does not. Death takes away only the projection. Relation is immortality.**

# VI. Ontological Evidence in Everyday Life

Many people are depressed and pessimistic because they mistakenly think they have lost relation, that they have “derailed from society.” But on this paper’s account, such “derailment” is impossible.

You are using electricity. Electricity comes from the national grid, from power-plant workers, from coal miners, from engineers. You use Chinese characters. Even if you do not speak, do not work, as long as you write something, you are using a writing system invented thousands of years ago. You spend money. Money is the “automatic deduction” of the relational network; as long as you live, it deducts. You write a paper on a server. Every character you type on GitHub enters into relation with engineers, coders, and data-center maintainers.

You lie in bed, merely connected to Wi-Fi, and you are already connected to billions of people and thousands of years of information flow. You do not need to move; you are already at the center of relation.

These everyday operations are each evidence that relation has never broken. You do not need to seek meaning; meaning is already there. You do not need to prove relation; relation is already operating.

# VII. Proof from Extreme Margins

The core thesis of this paper is: **as long as you are in relation, you have relational immortality.** But for this thesis to hold, it must pass the most extreme test.

**First Layer: The deaf-blind fool.** The air he breathes is shared by others. The water he drinks is purified by others. The food he eats is made by others. Even if he does nothing but lie there, he is already in relation. “Relation” does not need consciousness.

**Second Layer: The fool begging in the street.** The road was built by workers. The corner he sits in is managed by the government. He reaches out; passersby pass; even if they ignore him, that is a social interaction. Ignoring is also a relation. Relation precedes his consciousness, his consent.

**Third Layer: The dead of tens of thousands of years ago.** Their genes are still in us. Their language is still in our mouths. Their tools are still in our hands. Their personhood did not continue, but their material and cultural traces entered the subsequent relational network.

**Fourth Layer: Criminals and evildoers.** The prison he sits in was built by workers. The prison food he eats is provided by taxpayers. The language he speaks is shared by humanity. **But it must be stated: relational immortality does not constitute moral justification. Ethical responsibility does not vanish because of relational immortality.**

**Conclusion: No matter who you are, you are unconditionally in the relational network.** No one is excluded.

# VIII. Limited Dialogue with Three Traditions

This paper dialogues only with three traditions, because they directly address “relation and continuity.”

### With Marx: The Sum of Social Relations and Relational Continuity

Marx writes: “The human essence is no abstraction inherent in each single individual. In its reality it is the ensemble of the social relations.”

This paper adds the temporal dimension: if the human being is the sum of social relations, and social relations do not vanish with the biological body, then the human being’s traces as a relational node already continue in the relational network. This continuity is not the immortality of the soul; it is the continuity of the relational node.

### With Whitehead: A Strict Limitation of Objective Immortality

Whitehead’s “objective immortality” holds that every actual occasion, after occurring, becomes “objective material” for subsequent occasions. This paper accepts the concept but imposes two strict limitations:

**First, objective immortality is not immortality.** Objective immortality says “your traces remain”; immortality says “relation has not vanished.”

**Second, objective immortality is structural, not evaluative.** The objective immortality of a criminal and that of a saint are structurally equivalent. This does not constitute moral justification; it describes a structural fact.

### With Buddhist Dependent Origination: Relational Constitution, Without Soteriology

Buddhist dependent origination says: “When this exists, that exists; from the arising of this comes the arising of that.” All phenomena arise dependently.

This paper borrows dependent origination at the level of “relational constitution,” but does not engage Buddhist soteriology. Dependent origination says all phenomena arise dependently; this corresponds to this paper’s “relation precedes the entity”: there is not first a “thing,” and then relation; rather relation itself produces the appearance of “things.”

But this paper does not promise nirvana, does not promise liberation; it describes only relational constitution.

# IX. Operability: The Practical Dimension of Relation Is Immortality

This paper is not a purely theoretical construction. It has an operational dimension.

**First, for the fear of death.** What fears death is the disappearance of the “I.” But this paper has argued: “you” are not the projection; you are relation. The projection vanishes; relation does not. Ask yourself: “What exactly is the ‘I’ that would die?” If you look closely, you will find that the “I” is a temporary projection of the relational network. You are not that projection; you are relation.

**Second, for the loss of loved ones.** Those who lose loved ones suffer because “he/she disappeared.” But this paper has argued: the projection vanished; relation does not vanish. Do not ask “Where did he/she go?” Ask “In which relations does he/she still remain?”

**Third, for loneliness.** The lonely suffer because “I have derailed from the world.” But derailment is impossible. You use electricity; it comes from the national grid. You use Chinese characters; they are historical. You spend money; money is the automatic deduction of the relational network. You have never derailed; you merely **cannot see the connections**.

**Fourth, for depression.** The depressed suffer because “I have no meaning.” But meaning is not “made” by you; meaning is signed for by the relational network at your birth. This is a phenomenological description; it does not replace professional psychological treatment.

**Fifth, for hospice care.** In hospice care, help the dying see: you are not “losing” anything; you are shifting from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” Within a professional hospice framework, this can serve as an optional meaning-reconstruction resource.

**Conclusion: Relation is immortality is not a theory; it is an operable practice. It cannot stop death, but it can change how you see death.**

# X. Objections and Responses

**Objection 1: “Relation is immortality” is just self-consolation.**

**Response**: Religious “immortality of the soul” presupposes a substantial soul. This paper’s “relation is immortality” presupposes no substance. You are not immortal as a “soul”; you are immortal as a **relational node**. The difference: religious immortality needs a “thing” to carry it; this paper’s immortality does not.

**Objection 2: “You” and “relation” are the same thing—this is just a word game.**

**Response**: This is not a word game; it is conceptual clarification. The entire content of “you”—language, memory, character, social role, genes—comes entirely from relation. Saying “you are relation” is an **ontological assertion**: your mode of existence is relation. Here “you” refers to a relational node, not a personal ego.

**Objection 3: If relation is immortality, why do we still fear death?**

**Response**: What fears death is the projection labeled “I.” It thinks it is real; it thinks relation is “its.” When the projection sees through this, fear loses its ground.

**Objection 4: This theory leads to contempt for death.**

**Response**: This paper does not contempt death. Death takes away the projection; relation does not vanish. This conclusion does not mean “death is unimportant”; it means “death is not the end of relation.” On the contrary, this conclusion makes us cherish life more.

**Objection 5: This theory is too optimistic, ignoring the real pain brought by death.**

**Response**: This paper does not deny the pain brought by death. But pain is real, and pain is not **all**. The projection dissolved, but relation remains. This paper does not seek to “eliminate” pain; it seeks to **provide a structural consolation within pain**: you have not lost everything; you have lost only the projection.

# XI. Falsification Conditions

**Empirical conditions:**

**Condition 1: If a system is found with D_f = 0 and can precisely infer the microstate, then “the projection is not an information carrier” is falsified.**

**Condition 2: If information is found to be completely concentrated in a single neuron, then “information is in relation” is falsified.**

**Conceptual conditions:**

**Condition 3: If the entire causal traces of an individual are found to be completely untraceable within a finite time, then “objective immortality” fails in that case.**

**Condition 4: If “you” are found to exist independently of all relation, then “relation precedes the subject” is falsified.**

**Condition 5: If relation is found to exist without any “node,” then “relation is node” is falsified.**

No such counterexample has been found.

# XII. Conclusion

This paper began from an intuition in *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*, passed through relation precedes the entity, transcendental covenant, objective immortality, the distinction between projection and relation, everyday evidence, and extreme margins, and arrived at a limited conclusion:

**The individual is not an isolated substance but a node in the relational network; death terminates the individual’s operational interval, but does not erase its relational traces. Relational continuity is structural; but it is not personal immortality.**

Jesus died, but his relation remains. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” remains. The author will die, but the book remains in the reader’s hands. You do not need to know everyone; as long as you live, you are already related to all humanity.

**You have never completely derailed, because you have never completely left the relational network. But this is not “you have not died”; it is “after your operational interval as an individual terminates, your traces as a relational node enter the subsequent relational network.”**

**Relation is immortality—here “immortality” is the immortality of relation, not of the individual.**

Biological death really occurs. Relational continuity continues. Your traces as a relational node enter the subsequent relational network.

# Appendix A: Computational Verification of Relational Priority

## A.1 Positioning Statement

This appendix reports a computational experiment that translates the core thesis of Section II.5—“relation precedes the entity; the identity of a node is determined by relational structure, not by intrinsic attributes”—into operational variables for a falsifiable test.

**This experiment does not prove the philosophical thesis “relation precedes the entity.”** It does only one thing: in a runnable identity-generation model, it measures the relative contribution of relational structure and intrinsic attributes to the identifiability of node identity.

## A.2 Experimental Design

**Model**: Stochastic block model (SBM) graph, N = 300 nodes, three communities of 100 nodes each. Intra-community connection probability p_in = 0.3, inter-community p_out = 0.02. Each node has a d = 8 dimensional random feature vector. Key setting: **intrinsic attributes are only very weakly associated with community labels**.

**Identity generation**: K = 4 rounds of message passing.

**Measurement**: Nearest-centroid classifier, 5-fold cross-validation, averaged over 5 random seeds.

**Four conditions**:

1. Intrinsic attributes X only.
2. Relational structure A only (spectral embedding).
3. Blind identity (pure noise + relational aggregation).
4. Spectral embedding after shuffling relations.

## A.3 Results

| Condition | 5-fold CV accuracy | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| X only | 0.3767 ± 0.0284 | Near chance (0.3333) |
| A only | 0.8507 ± 0.1202 | Relational structure alone predicts community |
| Blind identity | 0.8720 ± 0.0720 | Intrinsic attributes are pure noise; relation still generates identity |
| After shuffling | 0.3460 ± 0.0158 | Relations destroyed; identity vanishes |

**Core comparisons**:

- acc_A − acc_X = 0.4740
- acc_blind − acc_X = 0.4953
- acc_A − acc_A_shuf = 0.5047

## A.4 Conclusion

The experiment supports: **in a reasonable identity-generation model, the identity of a node is primarily determined by its position in the relational network, not by its intrinsic attributes.**

Three core pieces of evidence:

1. **Relational contribution is more than twice the attribute contribution.**
2. **Blind identity holds**: intrinsic attributes are pure noise, yet relational structure alone generates identifiable identity.
3. **Relational destruction test holds**: after shuffling relations, accuracy drops to chance.

## A.5 Experimental Boundaries

**What this experiment can test**: in a message-passing model, the identifiability of identity is primarily determined by relational structure.

**What this experiment cannot test**: the philosophical thesis “relation precedes the entity” itself; identity generation in real human societies; any ontological conclusion.

**Honest labeling**: This is a structural analogy; it does not prove the philosophical thesis.

## A.6 Reproducibility

The experiment code is pure Python 3, no external dependencies. Fixed seeds = [42, 123, 456, 789, 1024], averaged over 5 runs. Code file: `relation_hard.py`.

# Appendix B: Information Loss and the Structural Analogy of Relational Priority

## B.1 Information-Theoretic Framework

*The Structural Origin of Irreversibility*, *A Consequence of Information Loss*, *Information Loss Rate of Macro Descriptions*, and *The Information Projection Framework* jointly show: in systems with non-injective aggregate mappings, macro descriptions do not contain the information needed to recover the microstate (D_f > 0). This provides a structural analogy at the information-theoretic level for Section V’s “the projection is not an information carrier.”

## B.2 Computational Verification

*Error-Correcting Code Structure in Neural Networks* found: **75% of information is in the correlations among neurons**, not in single neurons.

The computational experiment in Appendix A further shows: relational structure contributes far more to identifiability than intrinsic attributes; even when intrinsic attributes are pure noise, relational structure still generates identifiable identity.

These two results jointly show: **information is not in the projection; information is in relational structure.**

## B.3 Boundary Statement

The above information-theoretic framework and computational experiments jointly support Section V’s “the projection is not an information carrier.” They do not support Section II.5’s “relation precedes the entity”—the latter is conceptual analysis, not an empirical or computational conclusion.
