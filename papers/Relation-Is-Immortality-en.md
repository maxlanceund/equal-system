# Relation Is Immortality: From the Absolute Other to Relational Ontology

## Abstract

This paper is the sequel to *The Absolute Other: An Ontological Critique from Genetic Randomness to the Objectification of Consciousness* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026). The earlier work argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other: the body is a random assemblage of genes, consciousness is a derivative of the body, morality is a social fiction, and death is the termination of an operational interval. That conclusion was cold—it brought liberation, but also emptiness. This paper reverses it: if the “I” is the absolute other, then the “I” is part of relation.

The paper begins with an intuition from Keigo Higashino’s *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*: people die, but relation remains. It proposes a core thesis: **the human being is not a substance but a relational node; relation precedes the entity.**

**A note on “immortality.”** In this paper, “immortality” refers specifically to **relational immortality**: relation does not vanish when an individual’s operational interval terminates. **“Immortality” does not mean the continuation of individual consciousness, personal identity, or the soul; immortality is the immortality of relation, not of the individual.** The slogan “you have never died” uses “you” in the sense of a relational node, not a personal ego. Biological death really occurs.

The paper’s core argument proceeds in five steps. First, determination is relation: any entity that can be identified and determined must already be in relation. Second, relation precedes the entity: there is no entity “prior to relation”; any entity that can be determined must already be in relation. Third, the absolute other operates in the form of relation: genes, language, society, and death are relational forms, not isolated substances. Fourth, the transcendental covenant: the individual is thrown at birth into an unchosen relational network and cannot refuse to sign. Fifth, death as a corollary: death takes away the projection, not relation itself.

The paper uses everyday operations and extreme marginal cases to display the unconditionality of relation. It enters into dialogue with Marx, Jesus, Plato, Aristotle, Hume, Sartre, Wittgenstein, Nietzsche, Schopenhauer, Camus, Heidegger, Husserl, Levinas, Whitehead, Buddhism, Zhuangzi, cosmology, theology, Spinoza, Leibniz, Latour, Kant, Hegel, Descartes, Deleuze, physics, quantum mechanics, ethics, and political philosophy, reversing each in turn. The paper arrives at an ontological declaration: **relation precedes all. There is no substantial first cause; there is only relation holding. Death takes away the projection; relation itself does not vanish.**

**Keywords**: relational ontology · immortality · absolute other · transcendental covenant · objective immortality · relation precedes all

## 0. Terminology

The following core terms are used in this paper with strict definitions. After their first occurrence, the definitions govern all subsequent uses.

**Immortality (relational immortality)**: In this paper, “immortality” refers specifically to relational immortality—relation does not vanish when an individual’s operational interval terminates. **“Immortality” does not mean the continuation of individual consciousness, personal identity, or the soul; immortality is the immortality of relation, not of the individual.** The slogan “you have never died” uses “you” in the sense of a relational node / relation itself, not a biological individual or personal ego. Biological death really occurs.

**Relation precedes all**: “Precedes” here means constitutive priority, not temporal priority. Relation is not a new first substance; it is the mode of existence. To say “relation precedes all” is to say that any entity, in order to be identified and determined, must already be in relation.

**No first principle, only relation holding**: “No first principle” means there is no substantial first cause; it does not mean “relation is not a constitutive condition.” Relation is not a “thing”; relation is the mode of existence.

**Absolute other**: The constitutive conditions that an individual cannot autonomously choose. This paper does not claim “the absolute other is something outside relation”; it claims “the absolute other operates in the form of relation”—genes, language, society, and death are relational forms, not isolated substances.

**Transcendental covenant**: Not a transaction, but the relational network’s prior signing-for of the individual. You cannot refuse to sign, because refusal itself requires the support of the relational network.

**Projection**: The temporary presentation of the relational network on a particular carrier. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. The projection dissolves; relation does not.

**Objective immortality**: In Whitehead’s sense—traces become material for subsequent occasions. **Objective immortality ≠ personal immortality.** This paper uses the term only in the sense of “traces continuing to exist.”

**Relational node**: The position and function of an individual in the relational network. A node is not a “thing”; it is an intersection of the relational network. After the carrier terminates, the individual becomes a historical node / objective material.

**Operational interval**: The interval of experience in which a finite system produces meaning. Death is the termination of the operational interval.

# I. Introduction: Starting from an Intuition in *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*

When I read *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*, I felt something. Old Namiya dies, but his letters still affect the three thieves. Katsuro dies, but his “Rebirth” is still sung by Seri and heard by Harumi. Akiko dies, but Marukoen still shelters children. The author will die, but the book is still in the reader’s hands.

I followed this thing. It is not a “warm story”; it is harder than warmth. It is a structure: **people die, but relation remains.**

This intuition led me to a question: if the “I” is not a substance but relation, what exactly does death take away? If the “I” is constituted by relation from the start, then death takes away only the biological carrier; relation itself does not disappear. You do not live as a “thing”; you live as a **relational node**. The node may vanish biologically, but the relations it carries—the words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—still operate in the relational network.

This paper argues precisely this: **relation precedes the subject, existence precedes ability, meaning precedes seeking.** You are already in relation at birth. At death, relation does not break; it shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” You have never derailed, never been alone—here “you” refers to a relational node, not a personal ego. Biological death really occurs.

**This paper is the sequel to *The Absolute Other*, and its reversal.** The earlier work dismantled the “I” completely, until only an “un-kidnapped state” remained—free, but empty. This paper turns that emptiness inside out. What remains after the “I” is dismantled is not nothingness but **relation**. Relation is not a substitute for the “I”; relation is more original than the “I.” The earlier work was cold; this paper is warm. The earlier work was deconstruction; this paper is reconstruction. The earlier work said, “The other is dead, the I is born.” This paper says, “The other is dead, but relation is immortality.”

# II. Relation Precedes the Subject: Completing *The Absolute Other*

In *The Absolute Other*, I argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other. That which is labeled “I” comes entirely from outside; it is entirely relation.

But that conclusion was cold. It brought deconstruction, stripping, and the absolute freedom of “the other is dead, the I is born”—but also emptiness. For after dismantling the “I” completely, what remained was an “un-kidnapped state.” It was free, but empty.

Now I turn this conclusion to its other side: **if the “I” is the absolute other, then the “I” is part of relation. Since the “I” is relation, why remain troubled?**

**A key argument must be supplied: why is the “absolute other” relation?**

The earlier work argued that the “I” is the absolute other, but “absolute other” there meant **all externality**—genes, language, society, death. These externalities are not identical with relation. This paper must argue why the absolute other is relation:

- **Genes are relation**: a gene is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between parent and offspring. Without that relation, there is no gene.
- **Language is relation**: language is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between signifier and signified, speaker and listener. Without those relations, there is no language.
- **Society is relation**: society is not an isolated “thing”; it is relations among people. Without those relations, there is no society.
- **Death is relation**: death is not an isolated “thing”; it is the termination of an operational interval, the point at which relation shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” Without relation, there is no death.

Therefore, the absolute other is relation. It is not something “outside relation”; it is relation itself.

You are troubled because you think you are an independent “I,” and the “absolute other” threatens this “I.” But if you were never that “I,” and are instead relation itself, then what is there to be troubled about? Death cannot take away “you,” because “you” were always relation, and relation does not vanish with the biological carrier.

Thus *Relation Is Immortality* is not a negation of *The Absolute Other* but its **completion**. You first deconstruct the “I” completely; then you discover that what remains after deconstruction is not nothingness but the **relational network**. The relational network is not a substitute for the “I”; it is more original than the “I.”

You once said, “The human being is the relation of all people.” Now you push this to death: **a person dies, but remains the relation of all people.**

**The earlier work is deconstruction; this paper is reconstruction. The earlier work dismantled the “I”; this paper dismantles “loneliness.” The earlier work said, “You have nothing”; this paper says, “You have never lost anything.” The earlier work was cold; this paper is warm. But this paper’s warmth is not consolation; it is a structural statement: you are not immortal as a substance; you are immortal as a relational node. You have never been alone, because you have always been relation itself.**

## II.5 Relation Precedes the Entity

The preceding sections argued that “the absolute other operates in the form of relation.” But this is not yet enough. Someone may say: even if the “I” is constituted by relation, that only shows that the “I” as an entity depends on relation; it does not show that “relation precedes existence.” Relation might be a property of entities rather than their precondition.

This section gives a more precise argument. It does not concern “existence itself” (which this paper does not discuss); it concerns only the **entity**—that is, a concrete thing that can be identified and determined.

### Definitions

**Entity**: Something that can be identified and determined.

**Relation**: The structure of difference between two terms. The distinction between A and B is relation.

**Constitutive priority**: X precedes Y if and only if, for Y to be determined, X must already be presupposed.

### Argument

The word “entity” presupposes “identifiability.” A “thing” that cannot be identified by anything cannot be subsumed under the category “entity”—not because it does not exist, but because it is conceptually indistinguishable from “nothing.” Hegel already argued this at the opening of the *Science of Logic*: “pure being,” having no determination, is indistinguishable from “nothing.”

To identify something is to distinguish it from other things. Distinction is relation. Therefore, for something to be subsumed under the category “entity,” it must already be in relation.

This does not mean “relation is temporally prior to entities.” It means: relation is the constitutive condition for the concept “entity” to hold. Without relation, there is no category “entity.”

### The Limits of This Argument

This argument does not concern “existence itself.” It does not claim that “relation creates existence,” nor that “existence itself depends on relation.” It claims only: the concept “entity” presupposes relation.

In other words: this paper does not discuss whether “things-in-themselves” exist. It discusses only what we mean when we say “something exists.” We mean: something can be identified and determined. And “can be identified and determined” presupposes relation.

### Objections and Responses

**Objection 1: What if there is a “thing-in-itself” that exists but is never identified by anything?**

**Response**: This paper does not deny that such a “thing-in-itself” may exist. It points out only that it cannot be subsumed under the category “entity.” You may say it “exists,” but your “exists” has no determination; it equals “nothing.” This is not this paper’s arbitrary stipulation; it is the usage of the word “entity.”

**Objection 2: This argument only proves “relation is the condition of identification,” not “relation is the condition of existence.”**

**Response**: Correct. This paper claims only “relation is the constitutive condition of the entity,” not “relation is the condition of existence.” The former is conceptual analysis; the latter is an ontological assertion. This paper does not make the latter.

**Objection 3: This argument is circular.**

**Response**: This is not circular; it is conceptual analysis. The meaning of “entity” includes “identifiability.” This paper reveals this usage; it does not smuggle it in as a premise.

**Objection 4: If relation precedes the entity, is “relation” itself an entity?**

**Response**: This paper does not claim “relation is an entity.” Relation is not a “thing”; relation is the constitutive condition of entities. Asking “is relation an entity” is like asking “is red a color”—it confuses levels.

### The Place of This Argument in the Paper

This argument is the core of Section II. It completes what the preceding sections did not: the preceding sections argued that “the absolute other operates in the form of relation”; this argument proves that “relation is the constitutive condition of the entity.”

But the limits of this argument must be noted: it does not prove “relation precedes existence”; it proves only “relation precedes the entity.” The title’s “relation precedes all” is shorthand for “relation precedes the entity.” As for “existence itself,” this paper does not discuss it.

With this argument, Sections III (transcendental covenant), IV (death and objective immortality), and V (projection and relation) acquire a hard foundation: not because “relation is important,” but because “relation is the constitutive condition of the entity.” Therefore death can take away only the projection, never relation—because relation is the constitutive condition of the entity, not a property of the entity.

# III. The Transcendental Covenant: You Are Signed For at Birth

Christianity says that the relation between human beings and God is a covenant. I used to find this laughable, because it seemed like a transaction: you believe in him, he blesses you. A transaction presupposes two independent entities—party A and party B—each with its own interests and conditions, sitting down to negotiate.

But now I find it somewhat reasonable. For if you push “relation” to the ontological bottom, then “covenant” is no longer a legal metaphor but an **ontological description**.

In Christian theology, the core of “covenant” is not “I exchange conditions with you,” but **“I make a covenant with you, and bring you into my relation.”** God covenants with Abraham not because Abraham performed well, but because God actively draws Abraham into a relational network. This network precedes Abraham’s merit, precedes his descendants, even precedes his existence.

This is almost the same structure as “as long as you are born, you are already established.” The only difference is that Christianity names the source of this relational network “God,” while I name it “the sum of all relations.”

So the covenant is not “a human being negotiates conditions with God,” but “the human being is from the start incorporated into an irrevocable relational network.” You cannot choose birth, nor can you choose the language, culture, history, or genes into which you are born. You have been “covenanted.” You have been “signed for” by relation. The first breath you take is the signature of the covenant.

I call this the **transcendental covenant**. It is not a condition you negotiate with some God; it is the relational network’s “receipt” for you at birth. You have no signing right, because the act of signing is already part of relation. The only thing you can do is not to refuse the covenant, but to **acknowledge that it has already happened**.

You cannot refuse to sign, because “refusal” itself requires the support of the relational network—if you want to live in seclusion, you still need land, water, and air. You cannot exit, because as long as you are alive, you are using electricity, breathing, and using the language others left behind.

# IV. Death Is Not the Breaking of Relation: From Direct Participation to Objective Immortality

In *The Limits of Meaning*, I argued that meaning is a signal produced by a finite system through closed-loop operation within a finite interval of experience; outside the interval, meaning is undefined—not zeroed, but no longer defined.

Now I apply this framework to death. Death is the termination of the interval of experience. After death, the meaning of the “I”—if the “I” is a product of closed-loop operation—is likewise undefined.

But this is not the endpoint of this paper. This paper advances further: **what death takes away is the absolute other labeled “I,” not relation itself.**

You do not live as a “thing”; you live as a **relational node**. The node may vanish biologically, but the relations it carries—the words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—still operate in the relational network.

Whitehead has a concept called **“objective immortality.”** Every actual occasion, after it occurs, becomes “objective material” for subsequent occasions. Your life is a process; when it ends, you do not disappear—you become a **condition of subsequent processes**.

Thus death is not the breaking of relation, but relation’s shift from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” You are not immortal as a substance; you are immortal as **an event that has occurred**.

**A crucial clarification: “objective immortality” is not “immortality.”**

“Objective immortality” says, “Your traces remain.” “Immortality” says, “Relation has not vanished.” There is a conceptual slide between these two propositions.

- **Objective immortality**: your traces remain. The words you wrote are still read; the people you influenced still live.
- **Immortality (relational immortality)**: relation itself has not vanished. You have always been part of relation, and relation does not vanish with the disappearance of a node.

The difference is that traces are one layer of relation, not the whole of relation. You are “immortal” not because your traces remain, but because **you have always been relation itself, and relation is not a trace**. Traces may fade and be forgotten, but relation does not. For relation is the mode of existence itself, not a property of existence.

**The earlier work said: death is the termination of the operational interval; meaning is undefined outside the interval. This paper says: the interval terminates, but traces remain; meaning is undefined, but received meaning is still regenerated. The earlier work pushed death to the boundary; this paper finds relation beyond the boundary. The earlier work was cold because it saw only the termination of the interval; this paper is warm because it sees traces beyond the interval.**

# V. Projection and Relation: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation

This paper advances a thesis more radical than “death is not the breaking of relation”: **the “you” is not an independent substance at all, but only a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network.**

**Step One: You are not the master of relation; you are a node of relation.**

In *The Absolute Other*, this paper already argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other. Now this paper advances further: since the “I” is wholly constituted by relation, the “I” is not the master of relation but a **node** of relation. A node is not an independent entity; it is an intersection of the relational network. You think “you” are living; in fact the relational network is operating through this node.

**Step Two: The node is a temporary projection of the relational network.**

The node is not fixed. It changes with relation, and it vanishes with the biological body. But the vanishing of the node is not the vanishing of the relational network.

Use the metaphor of light: sunlight falls on water and forms a gleam. The gleam is not the water; it is only the reflection of sunlight on the water’s surface. You see the gleam and think it is an independent thing, but it is only a **temporary projection** of water, sunlight, and air. The sun leaves; the gleam vanishes; but the water remains, and the air remains.

“You” are like this. You are not an independent substance; you are a temporary projection of genes, language, society, history, and death.

**Step Three: The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network.**

This is the key distinction of this paper: **the projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network.**

Those who died tens of thousands of years ago—their projections vanished, but the information never vanished. For information was never in the projection; it was always in relation. Their genes are in your body; their language is in your mouth; their tools are in your hands. These are not “legacies they left behind”; they are **they themselves**. They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relation itself**.

**Step Four: The projection vanishes; relation remains.**

Therefore, death is not the vanishing of “you,” but the vanishing of the “projection.” You do not live as a substance; you live as the temporary presentation of the relational network. When the biological body dies, this mode of presentation terminates, but the relational network does not thereby break.

Jesus died, but his relation remains. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” remains. Keigo Higashino’s book remains in your hands. **They do not live as “souls”; they live as relational nodes. Their projections vanished, but their relation remains.**

**Conclusion: You are not you; you are the temporary projection of relation. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. Death takes away only the projection; relation itself has never vanished. You are still in this world, because you have always been the relation of this world.**

**Supplementary Section: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation—A Conceptual Clarification of “Immortality”**

This paper has argued that “you” are not an independent substance but a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. Now a key conceptual clarification is needed to answer a possible objection: **“Relation remains” does not equal “you remain.”**

**Objection**: You argue that relation does not vanish with death, but then you jump from “relation does not vanish” to “you have not died.” There is a conceptual slide. “Relation remains” does not equal “you remain.” Are “you” and “relation” the same thing? If not, the conclusion does not follow.

**Clarification: “You” never referred to the projection; “you” are relation.**

The answer is: **the word “you” never referred to the projection.** The projection is only a temporary mode of presentation; it is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. You mistakenly think “you” are that projection, and so you fear death. But once you see through this, you discover: the “you” that would die was never you. You are relation; relation is immortal.

This clarification has two steps:

**(a) The projection vanishes.** This paper never denied this. The biological carrier stops; the projection vanishes. This is a structural fact.

**(b) Relation does not vanish.** This paper has already argued this. The words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined still operate in the relational network. This too is a structural fact.

**(c) You are not the projection; you are relation.** This is the core thesis. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. You are not the projection that vanishes; you are the relation that does not vanish.

**Why are “you” relation rather than projection?** Because the entire content of “you”—your language, memory, character, social role, genes, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—comes entirely from relation and is entirely in relation. The projection is only a temporary form in which these relations appear. The form vanishes; relation does not.

Metaphor: a wave is a temporary form of seawater. The wave vanishes, but the seawater does not. You are not the wave; you are the seawater. The vanishing of the wave is not your vanishing.

**What does this mean for immortality?** “Relation is immortality” is not a jump from “relation remains” to “you remain.” It is:

- Premise 1: You are not the projection; you are relation.
- Premise 2: The projection vanishes; relation does not vanish.
- Conclusion: Death takes away only the projection; relation itself has never vanished.

This conclusion is not a leap; it is an analytic proposition. For the definition of “you” is relation; relation is immortal; therefore you are immortal. Here “you” refers to a relational node, not a personal ego.

**Conclusion: You are not the projection; you are relation. The projection vanishes; relation does not. Death takes away only the projection. Relation is immortality.**

# VI. Ontological Evidence in Everyday Life

Many people are depressed and pessimistic because they mistakenly think they have lost relation, that they have “derailed from society.” But on this paper’s account, such “derailment” is impossible.

You are using electricity. Electricity comes from the national grid, from power-plant workers, from coal miners, from engineers. You use Chinese characters. Even if you do not speak, do not work, as long as you write something, you are using a writing system invented thousands of years ago. You spend money. Money is the “automatic deduction” of the relational network; as long as you live, it deducts. You write a paper on a server. Every character you type on GitHub enters into relation with engineers, coders, and data-center maintainers.

You lie in bed, merely connected to Wi-Fi, and you are already connected to billions of people and thousands of years of information flow. You do not need to move; you are already at the center of relation.

These everyday operations are each evidence that relation has never broken. You do not need to seek meaning; meaning is already there. You do not need to prove relation; relation is already operating.

**The earlier work was cold because it told you: you have nothing; even the “I” is rented. This paper is warm because it tells you: you have never been alone; you have always been relation itself. The electricity you use, the money you spend, the Chinese characters you write—these are evidence that you are already in the relational network. You are not seeking relation; relation is you.**

# VII. Proof from Extreme Margins: From the Deaf-Blind Fool to the Beggar

The core thesis of this paper is: **as long as you are in relation, you have relational immortality.** But for this thesis to hold, it must pass the most extreme test. If it applies only to normal people, socially functional people, people who can speak and work, then it is a privilege theory. This paper rejects such privilege.

**First Layer: The deaf-blind fool.** The air he breathes is shared by others. The water he drinks is purified by others. The food he eats is made by others. The bed he lies in is made by others. Even if he does nothing but lie there, he is already in relation. For “lying there” itself requires a “there,” and “there” is provided by the relational network. His “consciousness” may not exist, but “relation” does not need consciousness.

**Second Layer: The fool begging in the street.** The road was built by workers. The corner he sits in is managed by the government. He reaches out; passersby pass; even if they ignore him, that is a social interaction. Ignoring is also a relation. He does not need to understand these interactions, agree to them, or even be aware of them. Relation precedes his consciousness, his consent, his understanding.

**Third Layer: The dead of tens of thousands of years ago.** Their genes are still in us. Their language is still in our mouths. Their tools are still in our hands. Their existence, as “events that have occurred,” is embedded in the causal chain of the universe. They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relational nodes**. Their projections vanished, but relation remains.

**Fourth Layer: Criminals and evildoers.** A murderer, a person despised by all society. He is still in relation. The prison he sits in was built by workers. The prison food he eats is provided by taxpayers. The language he speaks is shared by humanity. His genes come from his parents. He may be forgotten by history, abandoned by society, hated by all. But relation will not abandon him. For relation is not “others’ attitude toward him”; relation is **the mode of existence itself**. **But it must be stated: relational immortality does not constitute moral justification. Ethical responsibility does not vanish because of relational immortality.**

**Conclusion: No matter who you are, you are unconditionally in the relational network.** If the deaf-blind fool is in relation, then the depressed, unemployed, unsocial person is in relation; the bankrupt, divorced, abandoned person is in relation; the person lying in a rented room doing nothing but connecting to Wi-Fi is in relation; the poor wretch in Africa is in relation; the dead of tens of thousands of years ago are in relation; the criminal, the evildoer, the one hated by all—is in relation.

**No one is excluded. No exception. No condition. No threshold. You are born, and you are already in relation.**

**You cannot escape relation. You do not need to escape.**

# VIII. Dialogue with Philosophers

### With Marx: From “the Sum of Social Relations” to “Relational Immortality”

Marx writes in the sixth thesis on Feuerbach: “The human essence is no abstraction inherent in each single individual. In its reality it is the ensemble of the social relations.”

This is the starting point of this paper. But Marx said only half. He said the human being is the sum of social relations, but he did not say **how these relations continue in time**. This paper supplies the other half: if the human being is the sum of social relations, and social relations do not vanish with the biological body, then **the human being is already relationally immortal in relation**.

This immortality is not the immortality of the soul, not the immortality of consciousness, not the immortality of the “I.” It is the **immortality of the relational node**.

Jesus died, but his relation remains. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” remains. **They do not live as “souls”; they live as relational nodes.**

---

### With Jesus: The Transcendental Covenant and the Truth of Heaven

Jesus says that the human being is the relation between humanity and God. This paper reverses “covenant” from “a transaction between humanity and God” into “the relational network’s prior signing-for of the individual.”

Jesus did not die in vain. His primary meaning terminated within his interval—he was crucified, his body died. But his received meaning did not terminate. His disciples, Paul, Augustine, the entire Christian tradition, Western history, global civilization—all regenerate his meaning outside his interval. He did not die in vain, because his death itself became a key node in the relational network, still read and revalued two thousand years later.

On heaven and hell, Jesus says in Luke 17:21: “The kingdom of God is within you” (or “in your midst”). The core definition of heaven and hell in Christian theology is not spatial but **relational**: heaven is the relation of “being with God”; hell is the relation of “being separated from God.” Jesus says, “The kingdom of God is within you”; this paper says, “Relation is immortality.” Both redefine “immortality” from “an immortal soul-substance” to **the continuation of relation**.

### With Plato: No Need for Immortality in the World of Forms

Plato constructs a “world of Forms,” an eternal, perfect, transcendent realm. The soul comes from the world of Forms and is trapped in the body. True immortality is the soul’s return to the world of Forms.

This paper reverses this system: **the human being does not need immortality in the world of Forms. The human being is already relationally immortal; relation is immortal.**

The world of Forms is also relation. Where do “eternity,” “perfection,” and “transcendence” come from? They are spoken by **language**, by **society**, by **history**. They are not first principles; they are idealized forms presented by the relational network at a certain node.

The body is not a prison. The body is a relational node. It is constituted by the genes your parents gave you, the food you ate, the air you breathed, the tools you used. It is not a closed prison; it is an open relational node.

You do not need to “recollect” the Forms. You do not need “practice” to leave the body. You do not need to “pursue” transcendence. As long as you are in relation, you are already relationally immortal. The electricity you use, the money you spend, the Chinese characters you write—these are evidence that you are already in relation. Relation is not a property of the cosmos; relation is the premise of the cosmos. Relation holds; the cosmos is. Relation is the “mode of existence” of the cosmos, not its “creator.”

In this paper’s language: **the cosmos too is “signed for” by the relational network.** It cannot “refuse to sign,” because it is a node of the relational network. There is not first a “cosmos,” and then it enters relation; rather relation precedes the cosmos, and the cosmos is generated in relation.

**Step Two: The end of the cosmos is not entropy increase, but the continuation of relation.**

The second law of thermodynamics says the entropy of the universe is increasing. Eventually the universe will reach heat death. This is a pessimistic end: the death of the cosmos.

But this paper says: **entropy increase does not mean the death of the cosmos. For the relation of the cosmos has not died.**

What is entropy increase? It is a change in the “operational state” of the relational network. It is not the disappearance of the relational network; it is the relational network’s shift from “low-entropy ordered relation” to “high-entropy disordered relation.” Ordered relation and disordered relation are both relation. Heat death is not the end of relation; it is relation’s shift from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” The relation of the cosmos does not vanish; it merely changes from “active, low-entropy relation” to “quiescent, high-entropy relation.” But relation itself remains.

Therefore this paper’s cosmological conclusion is:

# X. The Ultimate Thesis: You Have Never Died

This paper has arrived at its ultimate thesis:

**The vanishing of the projection does not mean your vanishing. You are relation. You have not vanished.**

This is not rhetoric, not consolation, not a religious promise. It is a **structural statement**. This paper argues it in four steps.

“You” are not an independent substance but a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection is not an information carrier; information is in the relational network. Someone may say: if a person has no contribution, no descendants, no fame, and influences no one, does relation not break after death?

Every breath you take is exhaled by others. The water you drink is purified by others. The food you eat is grown by others. Even if you are silent all your life and do nothing, you are in relation.

**Therefore, even if you have no contribution, as long as you are in relation, you have not vanished.**

- Their language is still in our mouths.

They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relational nodes**. Their projections vanished, but relation remains.

**Step Four: Death takes away the projection; relation does not vanish.**

Now combine the four steps:

**You have not vanished. You have always been relation itself. Here “you” refers to a relational node, not a personal ego. Biological death really occurs.**

**Death takes away the projection; relation does not vanish. Relation is immortality.**

# Appendix A: Computational Verification of Relational Priority

## A.1 Positioning Statement

This appendix reports a computational experiment that translates the core thesis of Section II.5—“relation precedes the entity; the identity of a node is determined by relational structure, not by intrinsic attributes”—into operational variables for a falsifiable test.

**This experiment does not prove the philosophical thesis “relation precedes the entity.”** It proves no ontological conclusion. It does only one thing: in a runnable identity-generation model, it measures the relative contribution of relational structure and intrinsic attributes to the identifiability of node identity. The experiment tests structural logic, not ontological truth.

## A.2 Experimental Design

**Model**: Generate a stochastic block model (SBM) graph, N = 300 nodes, three communities of 100 nodes each. Intra-community connection probability p_in = 0.3, inter-community p_out = 0.02. Each node has a d = 8 dimensional random feature vector sampled from the standard normal distribution. Key setting: **intrinsic attributes are only very weakly associated with community labels** (signal strength 0.3, almost drowned in noise).

**Identity generation**: Use K = 4 rounds of message passing:

```

h_i^(0) = x_i
h_i^(t+1) = (1-α) h_i^(t) + α * mean_{j∈N(i)} h_j^(t)

```

Parameters: K = 4, α = 0.5.

**Measurement**: Nearest-centroid classifier, 5-fold cross-validation, predicting community labels. Averaged over 5 random seeds.

**Four conditions**:

1. **Intrinsic attributes X only**: direct classification.
2. **Relational structure A only**: spectral embedding (second eigenvector of adjacency matrix).
3. **Blind identity**: X replaced by pure random noise, then message passing; can relational structure alone generate identifiable identity?
4. **Spectral embedding after shuffling relations**: randomly rewire the graph, destroying community structure; does identity disappear?

## A.3 Results

| Condition | 5-fold CV accuracy (mean ± std) | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| X only | 0.3767 ± 0.0284 | Near chance (0.3333) |
| A only (spectral embedding) | 0.8507 ± 0.1202 | Relational structure alone predicts community |
| Blind identity (noise + relational aggregation) | 0.8720 ± 0.0720 | Intrinsic attributes are pure noise; relation still generates identity |
| Spectral embedding after shuffling | 0.3460 ± 0.0158 | Relations destroyed; identity vanishes; near chance |

**Core comparisons**:

- acc_A − acc_X = 0.4740 (relational contribution far exceeds attribute contribution)
- acc_blind − acc_X = 0.4953 (blind identity far exceeds attributes)
- acc_A − acc_A_shuf = 0.5047 (sharp drop after relational destruction)

## A.4 Conclusion

The experiment supports the following proposition: **in a reasonable identity-generation model, the identity of a node is primarily determined by its position in the relational network, not by its intrinsic attributes.**

Three core pieces of evidence:

1. **Relational contribution is more than twice the attribute contribution**: acc_A = 0.85 vs acc_X = 0.38.
2. **Blind identity holds**: intrinsic attributes are pure noise (acc_blind = 0.87), yet relational structure alone generates identifiable identity. This shows identity does not require intrinsic attributes as a foundation.
3. **Relational destruction test holds**: after shuffling relations, accuracy drops to chance (acc_A_shuf = 0.35). This shows relation is constitutive, not decorative.

## A.5 Experimental Boundaries

**What this experiment can test**:

- In a message-passing model, the identifiability of identity is primarily determined by relational structure.
- The contribution of intrinsic attributes to identifiability is near zero.
- When relational structure is destroyed, identifiability vanishes.

**What this experiment cannot test**:

- The philosophical thesis “relation precedes the entity” itself.
- Identity generation in real human societies.
- Any ontological conclusion.

**Honest labeling**: This is a structural analogy. It shows that, in a reasonable identity-generation model, the influence of relational structure on identity can far exceed that of intrinsic attributes, and even when intrinsic attributes are entirely absent, relational structure can still generate identifiable identity. This supports the intuition of “relation precedes the entity,” but does not prove it.

## A.6 Reproducibility

The experiment code is pure Python 3, no external dependencies, runnable on Termux. Fixed seeds = [42, 123, 456, 789, 1024], averaged over 5 runs. Code file: `relation_hard.py`. Run command: `python3 relation_hard.py`.

# Appendix B: Information Loss and the Structural Analogy of Relational Priority

## B.1 Information-Theoretic Framework

*The Structural Origin of Irreversibility* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026) argues: the non-injectivity of the aggregate mapping provides a static structural source for irreversibility. *A Consequence of Information Loss* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026) argues: coarse-grained macro descriptions do not contain the information needed to recover the microstate. *Information Loss Rate of Macro Descriptions* and *The Information Projection Framework* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026) define the information loss rate D_f = H(Ω|M′) / H(Ω) and prove: in systems with non-injective aggregate mappings, D_f > 0, and macro descriptions do not contain the information needed to recover the microstate.

This provides a structural analogy at the information-theoretic level for Section V’s “the projection is not an information carrier.” But it does not prove “relation precedes the entity”—the latter is conceptual analysis, not an information-theoretic conclusion.

## B.2 Computational Verification

*Error-Correcting Code Structure in Neural Networks* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026) uses D_f to quantitatively measure information distribution in hidden layers, finding: **75% of information is in the correlations among neurons**, not in single neurons; information is distributed across all neurons, not concentrated in a few “important” neurons; shuffling 40% of neurons loses only 6% of information; the error-correcting code is activated within the first training epoch.

The computational experiment in Appendix A further shows: in an identity-generation model, relational structure contributes far more to identifiability than intrinsic attributes (acc_A = 0.85 vs acc_X = 0.38); even when intrinsic attributes are pure noise, relational structure still generates identifiable identity (acc_blind = 0.87); after shuffling relations, identifiability drops to chance (acc_A_shuf = 0.35).

These two results jointly show: **information is not in the projection; information is in relational structure.** But they do not prove “relation precedes the entity”—the latter is conceptual analysis, not an information-theoretic or computational conclusion.

## B.3 Boundary Statement

The above information-theoretic framework and computational experiments jointly support Section V’s “the projection is not an information carrier.” They do not support Section II.5’s “relation precedes the entity”—the latter is conceptual analysis, not an empirical or computational conclusion. The strong claims of the related papers (the arrow of time is descriptive; the unification program does not need to explain the arrow of time) are outside this paper’s argumentative scope; this paper borrows only their formal framework to support the local proposition “the projection is not an information carrier.”
