# Relation Is Immortality: From the Absolute Other to Relational Ontology

> **Core Thesis**: This paper is the sequel to *The Absolute Other: An Ontological Critique from Genetic Randomness to the Objectification of Consciousness*, and a thorough reversal of it. The earlier work deconstructed the “I” into the absolute other, arriving at the cold conclusion “the other is dead, the I is born,” leaving a state of absolute freedom that was nevertheless empty. This paper reverses that conclusion: if the “I” is the absolute other, then the “I” is part of relation; since the “I” is relation, death takes nothing away. Meaning is not created by the subject; it is automatically conferred by the relational network at the subject’s birth. You are signed for at birth; at death, relation does not break—it merely shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” You are not a projection; you are relation. The projection vanishes; relation remains. You have never derailed, never been alone, never died. Starting from a felt intuition in Keigo Higashino’s *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*, this paper proceeds through five paths—relational ontology, transcendental covenant, objective immortality, everyday operational evidence, and extreme marginal cases—and enters into full dialogue with Marx, Jesus, Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Hegel, Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz, Hume, Nietzsche, Schopenhauer, Sartre, Camus, Deleuze, Latour, Wittgenstein, Heidegger, Husserl, Levinas, Whitehead, Buddhism, Zhuangzi, cosmology, theology, physics, and quantum mechanics. It arrives at an ontological declaration: relation precedes all. There is no first principle, no boundary, no absurdity, no pessimism, no abyss. There is only relation. Relation is immortality.

# I. Introduction: Starting from an Intuition in *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*

When I read *The Miracles of the Namiya General Store*, I felt something. Old Namiya dies, but his letters still affect the three thieves. Katsuro dies, but his “Rebirth” is still sung by Seri and heard by Harumi. Akiko dies, but Marukoen still shelters children. Keigo Higashino himself has died, but his book is still in my hands.

I followed this thing. It is not a “warm story”; it is harder than warmth. It is a structure: **people die, but relation remains.**

This intuition led me to a question: if the “I” is not a substance but relation, what exactly does death take away? If the “I” is constituted by relation from the start, then death takes away only the biological carrier; relation itself does not disappear. You do not live as a “thing”; you live as a **relational node**. The node may vanish biologically, but the relations it carries—the words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—still operate in the relational network.

This paper argues precisely this: **relation precedes the subject, existence precedes ability, meaning precedes seeking.** You are already in relation at birth. At death, relation does not break; it shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” You have never derailed, never been alone, never died.

**This paper is the sequel to *The Absolute Other*, and its thorough reversal.** The earlier work dismantled the “I” completely, until only an “un-kidnapped state” remained—free, but empty. This paper turns that emptiness inside out. What remains after the “I” is dismantled is not nothingness but **relation**. Relation is not a substitute for the “I”; relation is more original than the “I.” The earlier work was cold; this paper is warm. The earlier work was deconstruction; this paper is reconstruction. The earlier work said, “The other is dead, the I is born.” This paper says, “The other is dead, but relation is immortality.”

**Difference from existing traditions:**

- **Whitehead**: Whitehead speaks of “objective immortality,” but he means that an actual occasion becomes material for subsequent occasions. This paper means: you have always been relation itself, and relation does not vanish.
- **Buddhism**: Buddhism speaks of dependent origination, but its endpoint is nirvana. This paper’s endpoint is “relation is immortality.”
- **Spinoza**: Spinoza speaks of the eternity of substance, but he means that modes are eternal in substance. This paper means: you are substance itself.

# II. Relation Precedes the Subject: Completing *The Absolute Other*

In *The Absolute Other: An Ontological Critique from Genetic Randomness to the Objectification of Consciousness* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026), I argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other. The body is a random assemblage of genes, consciousness is a derivative of the body, morality is a social fiction, and death is the termination of an operational interval. That which is labeled “I” comes entirely from outside; it is entirely relation.

But that conclusion was cold. It brought deconstruction, stripping, and the absolute freedom of “the other is dead, the I is born”—but also emptiness. For after dismantling the “I” completely, what remained was an “un-kidnapped state.” It was free, but empty.

Now I turn this conclusion to its other side: **if the “I” is the absolute other, then the “I” is part of relation. Since the “I” is relation, why remain troubled?**

**A key argument must be supplied: why is the “absolute other” relation?**

The earlier work argued that the “I” is the absolute other, but “absolute other” there meant **all externality**—genes, language, society, death. These externalities are not identical with relation. This paper must argue why the absolute other is relation:

- **Genes are relation**: a gene is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between parent and offspring. Without that relation, there is no gene.
- **Language is relation**: language is not an isolated “thing”; it is a relation between signifier and signified, speaker and listener. Without those relations, there is no language.
- **Society is relation**: society is not an isolated “thing”; it is relations among people. Without those relations, there is no society.
- **Death is relation**: death is not an isolated “thing”; it is the termination of an operational interval, the point at which relation shifts from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” Without relation, there is no death.

Therefore, the absolute other is relation. It is not something “outside relation”; it is relation itself.

You are troubled because you think you are an independent “I,” and the “absolute other” threatens this “I.” But if you were never that “I,” and are instead relation itself, then what is there to be troubled about? Death cannot take away “you,” because “you” were always relation, and relation does not vanish with the biological carrier.

Thus *Relation Is Immortality* is not a negation of *The Absolute Other* but its **completion**. You first deconstruct the “I” completely; then you discover that what remains after deconstruction is not nothingness but the **relational network**. The relational network is not a substitute for the “I”; it is more original than the “I.”

You once said, “The human being is the relation of all people.” Now you push this to death: **a person dies, but remains the relation of all people.**

**The earlier work is deconstruction; this paper is reconstruction. The earlier work dismantled the “I”; this paper dismantles “loneliness.” The earlier work said, “You have nothing”; this paper says, “You have never lost anything.” The earlier work was cold; this paper is warm. But this paper’s warmth is not consolation; it is a structural statement: you are not immortal as a substance; you are immortal as a relational node. You have never been alone, because you have always been relation itself.**

# III. The Transcendental Covenant: You Are Signed For at Birth

Christianity says that the relation between human beings and God is a covenant. I used to find this laughable, because it seemed like a transaction: you believe in him, he blesses you. A transaction presupposes two independent entities—party A and party B—each with its own interests and conditions, sitting down to negotiate.

But now I find it somewhat reasonable. For if you push “relation” to the ontological bottom, then “covenant” is no longer a legal metaphor but an **ontological description**.

In Christian theology, the core of “covenant” is not “I exchange conditions with you,” but **“I make a covenant with you, and bring you into my relation.”** God covenants with Abraham not because Abraham performed well, but because God actively draws Abraham into a relational network. This network precedes Abraham’s merit, precedes his descendants, even precedes his existence.

This is almost the same structure as “as long as you are born, you are already established.” The only difference is that Christianity names the source of this relational network “God,” while I name it “the sum of all relations.”

So the covenant is not “a human being negotiates conditions with God,” but “the human being is from the start incorporated into an irrevocable relational network.” You cannot choose birth, nor can you choose the language, culture, history, or genes into which you are born. You have been “covenanted.” You have been “signed for” by relation. The first breath you take is the signature of the covenant.

I call this the **transcendental covenant**. It is not a condition you negotiate with some God; it is the relational network’s “receipt” for you at birth. You have no signing right, because the act of signing is already part of relation. The only thing you can do is not to refuse the covenant, but to **acknowledge that it has already happened**.

You cannot refuse to sign, because “refusal” itself requires the support of the relational network—if you want to live in seclusion, you still need land, water, and air. You cannot exit, because as long as you are alive, you are using electricity, breathing, and using the language others left behind.

# IV. Death Is Not the Breaking of Relation: From Direct Participation to Objective Immortality

In *The Limits of Meaning: A Verificationist Framework* (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026), I argued that meaning is a signal produced by a finite system through closed-loop operation within a finite interval of experience; outside the interval, meaning is undefined—not zeroed, but no longer defined.

Now I apply this framework to death. Death is the termination of the interval of experience. After death, the meaning of the “I”—if the “I” is a product of closed-loop operation—is likewise undefined. This is not to say that the “I” disappears, but that the function “I” has no value outside its domain.

But this is not the endpoint of this paper. This paper advances further: **what death takes away is the absolute other labeled “I,” not relation itself.**

You do not live as a “thing”; you live as a **relational node**. The node may vanish biologically, but the relations it carries—the words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—still operate in the relational network.

Whitehead has a concept called **“objective immortality.”** Every actual occasion, after it occurs, becomes “objective material” for subsequent occasions. Your life is a process; when it ends, you do not disappear—you become a **condition of subsequent processes**. Even if you have no children, every action you performed, every word you spoke, even your silence, has entered the causal chain of the universe and become “objective material” that future events cannot erase.

Thus death is not the breaking of relation, but relation’s shift from “direct participation” to “objective immortality.” You are not immortal as a substance; you are immortal as **an event that has occurred**. You do not need descendants, fame, or a soul to obtain immortality, because as “an event that has occurred,” you are already embedded in the causal structure of the universe.

**A crucial clarification: “objective immortality” is not “immortality.”**

“Objective immortality” says, “Your traces remain.” “Immortality” says, “You have not died.” There is a conceptual slide between these two propositions.

- **Objective immortality**: your traces remain. The words you wrote are still read; the people you influenced still live.
- **Immortality**: you have not died. You have always been relation itself, and relation does not vanish.

The difference is that traces are one layer of relation, not the whole of relation. You are “immortal” not because your traces remain, but because **you have always been relation itself, and relation is not a trace**. Traces may fade and be forgotten, but relation does not. For relation is the mode of existence itself, not a property of existence.

**The earlier work said: death is the termination of the operational interval; meaning is undefined outside the interval. This paper says: the interval terminates, but traces remain; meaning is undefined, but received meaning is still regenerated. The earlier work pushed death to the boundary; this paper finds relation beyond the boundary. The earlier work was cold because it saw only the termination of the interval; this paper is warm because it sees traces beyond the interval.**

# V. Projection and Relation: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation

This paper advances a thesis more radical than “death is not the breaking of relation”: **the “you” is not an independent substance at all, but only a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection contains no information and no meaning. All information is in relation.**

**Step One: You are not the master of relation; you are a node of relation.**

In *The Absolute Other*, this paper already argued that the “I” is not a subject but the absolute other. The body is a random assemblage of genes, consciousness is a derivative of the body, morality is a social fiction. That which is labeled “I” comes entirely from outside; it is entirely relation.

Now this paper advances further: since the “I” is wholly constituted by relation, the “I” is not the master of relation but a **node** of relation. A node is not an independent entity; it is an intersection of the relational network. You think “you” are living; in fact the relational network is operating through this node.

**Step Two: The node is a temporary projection of the relational network.**

The node is not fixed. It changes with relation, and it vanishes with the biological body. But the vanishing of the node is not the vanishing of the relational network.

Use the metaphor of light: sunlight falls on water and forms a gleam. The gleam is not the water; it is only the reflection of sunlight on the water’s surface. You see the gleam and think it is an independent thing, but it is only a **temporary projection** of water, sunlight, and air. The sun leaves; the gleam vanishes; but the water remains, and the air remains.

“You” are like this. You are not an independent substance; you are a temporary projection of genes, language, society, history, and death.

**Step Three: The projection contains no information; all information is in relation.**

This is the key distinction of this paper: **the projection contains no information and no meaning. All information is in relation.**

Those who died tens of thousands of years ago—their projections vanished, but the information never vanished. For information was never in the projection; it was always in relation. Their genes are in your body; their language is in your mouth; their tools are in your hands. These are not “legacies they left behind”; they are **they themselves**. They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relation itself**.

**Step Four: The projection vanishes; relation remains.**

Therefore, death is not the vanishing of “you,” but the vanishing of the “projection.” You do not live as a substance; you live as the temporary presentation of the relational network. When the biological body dies, this mode of presentation terminates, but the relational network does not thereby break.

Jesus died, but his relation remains. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” remains. Keigo Higashino died, but his book remains in your hands. **They do not live as “souls”; they live as relational nodes. Their projections vanished, but their relation remains.**

**Conclusion: You are not you; you are the temporary projection of relation. The projection contains no information; all information is in relation. Death takes away only the projection; relation itself has never vanished. You are still in this world, because you have always been the relation of this world.**

**Supplementary Section: You Are Not the Projection; You Are Relation—A Conceptual Clarification of “Immortality”**

This paper has argued that “you” are not an independent substance but a temporary projection of the relational network. The projection contains no information; all information is in relation. Now a key conceptual clarification is needed to answer a possible objection: **“Relation remains” does not equal “you remain.”**

**Objection**: You argue that relation does not vanish with death, but then you jump from “relation does not vanish” to “you have not died.” There is a conceptual slide. “Relation remains” does not equal “you remain.” Are “you” and “relation” the same thing? If not, the conclusion does not follow.

**Clarification: “You” never referred to the projection; “you” are relation.**

The answer is: **the word “you” never referred to the projection.** The projection is only a temporary mode of presentation; it contains no information; all information is in relation. You mistakenly think “you” are that projection, and so you fear death. But once you see through this, you discover: the “you” that would die was never you. You are relation; relation is immortal.

This clarification has two steps:

**(a) The projection vanishes.** This paper never denied this. The biological carrier stops; the projection vanishes. This is a structural fact.

**(b) Relation does not vanish.** This paper has already argued this. The words you wrote, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined still operate in the relational network. This too is a structural fact.

**(c) You are not the projection; you are relation.** This is the core thesis. The projection contains no information; all information is in relation. You are not the projection that vanishes; you are the relation that does not vanish.

**Why are “you” relation rather than projection?** Because the entire content of “you”—your language, memory, character, social role, genes, the people you influenced, the conversations you joined—comes entirely from relation and is entirely in relation. The projection is only a temporary form in which these relations appear. The form vanishes; relation does not.

Metaphor: a wave is a temporary form of seawater. The wave vanishes, but the seawater does not. You are not the wave; you are the seawater. The vanishing of the wave is not your vanishing.

**What does this mean for immortality?** “Relation is immortality” is not a jump from “relation remains” to “you remain.” It is:

- Premise 1: You are not the projection; you are relation.
- Premise 2: The projection vanishes; relation does not vanish.
- Conclusion: Death takes away only the projection; relation itself has never vanished. Therefore you have not died.

This conclusion is not a leap; it is an analytic proposition. For the definition of “you” is relation; relation is immortal; therefore you are immortal. This is not consolation; it is conceptual clarification.

**Conclusion: You are not the projection; you are relation. The projection vanishes; relation does not. Death takes away only the projection; you have never died. Relation is immortality.**

# VI. Ontological Evidence in Everyday Life

Many people are depressed and pessimistic because they mistakenly think they have lost relation, that they have “derailed from society.” But on this paper’s account, such “derailment” is impossible. The evidence lies in the most ordinary reality.

**You are using electricity.** Electricity does not fall from the sky. It comes from the national grid, from power-plant workers, from coal miners, from engineers who designed the lines, from countless people you do not know cooperating. As long as you use electricity, you are in relation with the whole society. You do not know them, but they are already in your existence.

**You pay the electricity bill.** The act of paying is itself your acknowledgment and maintenance of this relation. You are not an island; you are a node in the relational network, paying and being supplied.

**You use Chinese characters.** Even if you do not speak, do not work, as long as you write something, you are using a writing system invented thousands of years ago. Every character you use is historical, used by countless people. In writing Chinese, you are in dialogue with the entire history of the Chinese language.

**You spend money.** You are hungry; you buy; you pay. You do not even need to think “I am entering social relation,” but relation has already occurred. Money is the “automatic deduction” of the relational network. As long as you live, it deducts. You cannot escape.

**You write a paper on an American server.** You write on GitHub; the server is American; Americans store your data. Who built the server? American workers. You do not need to know them, speak English, or visit America. But every character you type on GitHub enters into relation with American engineers, coders, and data-center maintainers. They do not know you; you do not know their names; but their labor is in your paper.

**You lie in bed and know you are connected to the network.** You do nothing, just lie there, but you know your phone is connected to Wi-Fi. Wi-Fi was set up by others; servers are maintained by others; the internet is the crystallization of all human history. You lie there, but you are already connected to billions of people and thousands of years of information flow. You do not need to move; you are already at the center of relation.

These everyday operations are each evidence that relation has never broken. You do not need to seek meaning; meaning is already there. You do not need to prove relation; relation is already operating.

**The earlier work was cold because it told you: you have nothing; even the “I” is rented. This paper is warm because it tells you: you have never been alone; you have always been relation itself. The electricity you use, the money you spend, the Chinese characters you write—these are evidence that you are already in the relational network. You are not seeking relation; relation is you.**

# VII. Proof from Extreme Margins: From the Deaf-Blind Fool to the Beggar

The core thesis of this paper is: **as long as you are in relation, you are immortal.** But for this thesis to hold, it must pass the most extreme test. If it applies only to normal people, socially functional people, people who can speak and work, then it is a privilege theory. This paper rejects such privilege. It pushes the thesis to the most extreme margin: **even if a person is deaf, blind, and a fool, his relation still holds; he is still immortal.**

**First Layer: The deaf-blind fool.**

Suppose a person is born deaf and blind, with severe intellectual disability. He cannot speak, understand language, recognize others, or live independently. By social standards, he is “meaningless”—no output, no contribution, no sociality, not even consciousness. He appears to be the most complete “derailer.”

But this paper asks: **Has he really derailed?**

The air he breathes is shared by others. The water he drinks is purified by others. The food he eats is made by others. The bed he lies in is made by others. Even if he does nothing but lie there, he is already in relation. For “lying there” itself requires a “there,” and “there” is provided by the relational network.

Further: his genes come from his parents; his body is composed of cosmic elements; his existence is permitted by the second law of thermodynamics. He is not an isolated entity; he is a node of the relational network. His “consciousness” may not exist, but “relation” does not need consciousness. Relation needs only existence itself.

**Second Layer: The fool begging in the street.**

Suppose this deaf-blind fool is placed on the street to beg. He sits by the road, reaches out, or simply lies there. He does not know he is begging, does not know what money is, does not know who passersby are.

But the road was built by workers. The corner he sits in is managed by the government. He reaches out; passersby pass; even if they ignore him, that is a social interaction. Ignoring is also a relation, for ignoring means “being seen but not responded to,” which is itself social.

Even a coin, a glance, or an act of ignoring is a social interaction. He does not need to understand these interactions, agree to them, or even be aware of them. Relation precedes his consciousness, his consent, his understanding.

**Third Layer: The dead of tens of thousands of years ago.**

People tens of thousands of years ago have long turned to dust. They have no names, no graves, no descendants who remember them. By ordinary standards, they “completely disappeared.”

But this paper asks: **Did they really disappear?**

- Their genes are still in us. The gene pool of modern humans is a mixture of countless people from tens of thousands of years ago.
- Their language is still in our mouths. Every word we speak is historical.
- Their tools are still in our hands. Every tool we use is a continuation of history.
- Their existence, as “events that have occurred,” is embedded in the causal chain of the universe.

They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relational nodes**. Their projections vanished, but relation remains. They are still in this world, because they have always been the relation of this world.

**Fourth Layer: Criminals and evildoers.**

Now take the most extreme example: a murderer, a rapist, a person despised by all society. By moral standards, he is “evil.” By social standards, he should be excluded. By this paper’s standards?

This paper says: **he too is already immortal.**

Why? Because he is still in relation. The prison he sits in was built by workers. The prison food he eats is provided by taxpayers. The language he speaks is shared by humanity. His genes come from his parents. His existence, as “an event that has occurred,” is embedded in the causal chain of the universe.

He may be forgotten by history, abandoned by society, hated by all. But relation will not abandon him. For relation is not “others’ attitude toward him”; relation is **the mode of existence itself**. He lives, and so he is in relation. He dies, the projection dissolves, but relation does not vanish.

**He is not immortal because he is “good”; he is immortal because he “is.”**

**Conclusion: No matter who you are, you are already immortal.**

If the deaf-blind fool is in relation and is immortal, then:

- The depressed, unemployed, unsocial person is in relation, and is immortal.
- The bankrupt, divorced, abandoned person is in relation, and is immortal.
- The person lying in a rented room doing nothing but connecting to Wi-Fi is in relation, and is immortal.
- The person writing a paper on an American server is in relation, and is immortal.
- The poor wretch in Africa is in relation, and is immortal.
- The dead of tens of thousands of years ago are in relation, and are immortal.
- The criminal, the evildoer, the one hated by all—is in relation, and is immortal.

**No one is excluded. No exception. No condition. No threshold. You are born, and you are already immortal.**

**You cannot escape relation. You do not need to escape.**

# VIII. Dialogue with Philosophers

### With Marx: From “the Sum of Social Relations” to “Relation Is Immortality”

Marx writes in the sixth thesis on Feuerbach: “The human essence is no abstraction inherent in each single individual. In its reality it is the ensemble of the social relations.”

This is the starting point of this paper. But Marx said only half. He said the human being is the sum of social relations, but he did not say **how these relations continue in time**. This paper supplies the other half: if the human being is the sum of social relations, and social relations do not vanish with the biological body, then **the human being is already immortal in relation**.

The argument:

- Premise 1: The human being = the sum of social relations. (Marx)
- Premise 2: Social relations do not vanish with the biological body; they shift from direct participation to objective immortality. (This paper’s use of Whitehead)
- Conclusion: The human being is already immortal in relation. (This paper’s advance)

This immortality is not the immortality of the soul, not the immortality of consciousness, not the immortality of the “I.” It is the **immortality of the relational node**. You do not need descendants, fame, or a soul to obtain immortality, because as “an event that has occurred,” you are already embedded in the causal structure of the universe.

Jesus died, but his relation remains—his disciples, his words, his story are still read two thousand years later. Katsuro died, but his “Rebirth” is still sung. Keigo Higashino died, but his book is still in your hands. **They do not live as “souls”; they live as relational nodes.**

So Marx was half right. He said the human being is the sum of social relations. This paper adds the other half: **the human being is the sum of social relations, and social relations do not vanish. Therefore the human being is already immortal in relation.**

### With Jesus: The Transcendental Covenant and the Truth of Heaven

Jesus says that the human being is the relation between humanity and God. This paper reverses “covenant” from “a transaction between humanity and God” into “the relational network’s prior signing-for of the individual.” Jesus did not die in vain. His primary meaning terminated within his interval—he was crucified, his body died. But his received meaning did not terminate. His disciples, Paul, Augustine, the entire Christian tradition, Western history, global civilization—all regenerate his meaning outside his interval. # XII. Objections and Responses

**Objection 1: “Relation is immortality” is just self-consolation, no different from religious “immortality of the soul.”**

**Response**: This paper differs fundamentally from religious “immortality of the soul.” Religious immortality presupposes a substantial soul that continues after bodily death. It requires an extra ontological commitment: the soul is a “thing” that can exist independently of the body. This paper’s “relation is immortality” presupposes no substance. It is not a secular version of “immortality of the soul”; it is a necessary conclusion of relational ontology. You are not immortal as a “soul”; you are immortal as a **relational node**. You are not immortal because there is an immortal “you”; you are immortal because **you have always been relation itself**. The difference: religious immortality needs a “thing” to carry it; this paper’s immortality does not. Relation is not a “thing”; relation is the mode of existence itself. So this paper’s immortality is not consolation; it is a **structural statement**.

**Objection 2: “You” and “relation” are the same thing—this is just a word game.**

**Response**: This is not a word game; it is conceptual clarification. The word “you” in everyday language usually refers to the conscious, embodied, socially situated individual. This paper does not deny this everyday usage. Its argument is: that which everyday language labels “you” is wholly constituted by relation. Its language comes from relation, its memory from relation, its character from relation, its social role from relation. It is not first an independent “you,” and then entering relation; it is relation converging at your node. So saying “you are relation” is not a word game; it is an **ontological assertion**: your mode of existence is relation. You do not “have” relation; you “are” relation. This is not a linguistic substitution; it is an ontological redescription.

**Objection 3: The dead of tens of thousands of years ago—their relations have long dissolved.**

**Response**: This paper has responded in Section VII. Their **direct relations** may have dissolved (the people they knew died, the conversations they joined ended). But their **objective immortality** has not dissolved. Their genes are still in us; their language is still in our mouths; their tools are still in our hands; their existence, as “events that have occurred,” is embedded in the causal chain of the universe. They do not live as “souls”; they live as **relational nodes**. Their projections vanished, but relation remains. They are still in this world, because they have always been the relation of this world.

**Objection 4: This is pantheism—turning everything into “relation” says nothing.**

**Response**: This paper is not pantheism. Pantheism says “God is nature”; this paper says “relation precedes all.” The difference: pantheism presupposes a “God” as first principle; this paper presupposes no first principle. This paper’s “relation” is not a “thing,” not a “substance,” not a “God.” It is **the mode of existence itself**. To say “relation precedes all” is not to say “relation is a thing that precedes all,” but “the mode of existence of all things is relation.” This is not an ontological commitment; it is an **ontological description**. As for “saying nothing”—this paper has operational definitions (D_f, non-injectivity, fiber, objective immortality), verifiable empirical evidence (five experimental papers), and an operable practical dimension (Section XI). It is not empty talk; it is a framework with formal foundations, empirical support, and practical application.

**Objection 5: This theory cannot be falsified, so it is not scientific.**

**Response**: This paper is not a scientific theory; it is a philosophical theory. The standard for philosophical theory is not “falsifiability” but “conceptual clarity, argumentative rigor, explanatory power.” But this paper’s theory is not entirely unfalsifiable. Section XIII gives explicit falsification conditions—empirical and conceptual. They need not be “scientific experiments”; they need only be “testable empirical propositions” and “testable conceptual propositions.”

**Objection 6: If relation is immortality, why do we still fear death?**

**Response**: What fears death is the projection labeled “I.” It thinks it is real; it thinks relation is “its.” But the projection was never real, and relation was never “its.” When the projection sees through this, fear loses its ground. Fear of death is not because death is truly terrifying, but because **the projection mistakenly thinks it will die**. Just as a wave fears vanishing because it mistakes itself for the wave rather than the seawater. When the wave sees it is seawater, it no longer fears. This paper does not deny that fear of death is a real psychological phenomenon. It says only: this fear has a cognitive root—**misidentification**. When this misidentification is seen through, fear loses its ground.

**Objection 7: This theory leads to contempt for death, even encourages suicide.**

**Response**: This paper does not contempt death, nor encourage suicide. It says only: death takes away only the projection; relation does not vanish. This conclusion does not mean “death is unimportant”; it means “death is not the end.” On the contrary, this conclusion makes us cherish life more. For if you know that every action, every word, every interaction will be permanently embedded in the relational network, you will treat them more seriously. You are not “waiting for death”; you are **participating in the operation of the relational network**. Every choice has irrevocable weight. This paper does not encourage suicide. It encourages: **see through the cognitive root of the fear of death, and then live more seriously in relation.**

**Objection 8: This theory is too optimistic, ignoring the real pain brought by death.**

**Response**: This paper does not deny the pain brought by death. Those who lose loved ones suffer; those facing their own death fear. This pain is real. But this paper says: pain is real, but pain is not **all**. The projection dissolved, but relation remains. Your relation with your loved one does not break because the biological carrier vanished. This paper does not seek to “eliminate” pain; it seeks to **provide a structural consolation within pain**: you have not lost everything; you have lost only the projection. Relation remains. You remain. He/she remains.

**Conclusion: This paper’s core thesis—“relation precedes all, relation is immortality”—withstands these objections. It is not self-consolation, not a word game, not pantheism, not unfalsifiable, not contemptuous of death, and not neglectful of pain. It is a philosophical framework with formal foundations, empirical support, and practical dimensions.**

# XIII. Falsification Conditions

If “relation is immortality” is an operable thesis, it should be falsifiable. This paper gives explicit falsification conditions, distinguishing **empirical** and **conceptual**.

**Empirical conditions:**

**Condition 1: If a system is found with D_f = 0 and can precisely infer the microstate, then “the projection contains no information” is falsified.** This paper defines information loss rate D_f = H(Ω|M′)/H(Ω) in *The Information Projection Framework*. If D_f = 0, the projection is injective, and the microstate can be precisely recovered from the macro description. If such a system exists, the thesis “the projection contains no information” does not hold. But this paper has argued: the composite projection `g ∘ f` is non-injective, so D_f > 0. If a macro description M′ with D_f = 0 is found, the framework is falsified.

**Condition 2: If information is found to be completely concentrated in a single neuron, then “information is in relation” is falsified.** In *Error-Correcting Code Structure in Neural Networks*, this paper found that 75% of information is in the correlations among neurons, not in single neurons. If a network is found with information completely concentrated in a single neuron, the thesis “information is in relation” does not hold.

**Condition 3: If D_f is found to be time-asymmetric under reversible dynamics, then “D_f does not capture the arrow of time” is falsified.** In *Information Loss Rate of Macro Descriptions*, this paper reports a negative result: D_f is time-symmetric under frictionless (reversible) dynamics. If D_f is found to be time-asymmetric under reversible dynamics, this negative result is overturned.

**Conceptual conditions:**

**Condition 4: If the relations of the dead of tens of thousands of years ago are found to have completely dissolved, then “objective immortality” is falsified.** This paper argues: their genes are still in us, their language is still in our mouths, their tools are still in our hands. If the traces of a person from tens of thousands of years ago are found to have completely disappeared, the argument is falsified.

**Condition 5: If “you” are found to exist independently of relation, then “relation precedes the subject” is falsified.** This paper argues: you are not an independent substance; you are a relational node. If a “you” is found that can exist independently of all relation, the core thesis is falsified.

**Condition 6: If “relation” is found to exist without any “node,” then “relation is node” is falsified.** This paper argues: relation is not a “thing”; relation is the mode of existence. If relation is found to exist without any node, the framework is falsified.

**Conclusion: This paper’s thesis is not unfalsifiable. It has explicit falsification conditions—empirical and conceptual. These conditions have not been met, so the thesis holds for now.**

# XIV. Dialogue with Contemporary Philosophy

This paper has dialogued with almost all important philosophers from Plato to Deleuze, but the list stops at the twentieth century. Now it enters dialogue with contemporary philosophy (twenty-first century).

### With Speculative Realism

Speculative Realism is a philosophical movement that emerged in the early twenty-first century, represented by Quentin Meillassoux, Graham Harman, Ray Brassier, and Iain Hamilton Grant. Its core claim: philosophy should escape “correlationism”—the view that we can know only the correlation between thought and being, not being itself.

This paper’s relation to Speculative Realism is complex. On the one hand, it agrees with the critique of correlationism: relation is not “the relation between thought and being”; relation is the mode of existence itself. On the other hand, it does not hold that “being itself” is a “thing” independent of relation. Meillassoux speaks of the “Great Outdoors,” a reality independent of thought. This paper says: that “Great Outdoors” is also relation. It is not a “thing” independent of relation; it is the presentation of the relational network at a certain node. Thus this paper is more radical: not only thought is relation, but being is relation. Not “relation is the relation between thought and being,” but “relation precedes thought and being.”

### With New Materialism

New Materialism is another movement of the early twenty-first century, represented by Karen Barad, Jane Bennett, and Rosi Braidotti. Its core claim: matter is not passive, inert; matter is active and relational.

This paper is highly consistent with New Materialism. Barad’s concept of “intra-action” is almost the same thesis as “relation precedes the subject.” She says: there are not first “things,” and then interaction; rather interaction itself produces the boundaries of “things.” This corresponds exactly to “relation precedes all.” But there is a difference: New Materialism mainly discusses the relationality of matter; this paper discusses **the relationality of existence itself**. Matter is relation, consciousness is relation, language is relation, society is relation, death is relation. Relation is not a property of matter; relation is the mode of existence.

### With Object-Oriented Ontology

Object-Oriented Ontology (OOO) is proposed by Graham Harman. Its core claim: objects are independent and irreducible; relations among objects are not “interaction” but “vicarious causation.”

This paper fundamentally disagrees with OOO. OOO says objects are independent and irreducible. This paper says objects are relational nodes, not independent substances. OOO says relations among objects are “vicarious.” This paper says relation precedes objects; relation is not “vicarious”; relation is the mode of existence itself. Harman might ask: if everything is relation, what is “relation” itself? Is it an “object”? This paper answers: relation is not an “object”; relation is the mode of existence. It is not “a thing”; it is the mode in which “things” exist. This is not OOO’s “object”; it is the premise of OOO’s “object.”

### With Posthumanism

Posthumanism is an important current of the twenty-first century, represented by Donna Haraway, Katherine Hayles, and Rosi Braidotti. Its core claim: the human is not the center, not independent; the human is entangled with nature, technology, and others.

This paper is highly consistent with Posthumanism. Haraway’s “cyborg” is almost the same thesis as “relational node.” She says: we are all cyborgs; we are all hybrids of organism and machine; we are all relational beings. But there is a difference: Posthumanism mainly discusses the relationality of the human; this paper discusses **the relationality of existence itself**. Not only the human is relation; the cosmos is relation, God is relation, death is relation. Relation is not a property of the human; relation is the mode of existence.

**Conclusion: This paper is highly consistent with contemporary philosophy (Speculative Realism, New Materialism, OOO, Posthumanism) on “relation precedes the subject,” but more radical on “relation precedes all.” Not “the human is relation,” but “existence is relation.” Not “relation is the mode of existence,” but “relation is existence itself.”**

# Appendix: Core Support from Five Experimental Papers

This paper’s core thesis—“the projection contains no information; all information is in relation; death is only the dissolving of the projection; relation is immortality”—is not a product of philosophical speculation. It is built on five experimental papers. These five papers use information theory, statistical mechanics, numerical simulation, and real systems to provide formal, quantifiable, and falsifiable scientific support.

**“The Structural Origin of Irreversibility”** (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026a) argues: the non-injectivity of the aggregate mapping provides a **static structural source** for irreversibility. The impossibility of recovering the microstate from the macro description does not depend on dynamical evolution, initial conditions, or randomness. It is a purely structural fact.

**“A Consequence of Information Loss”** (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026b) argues: the predicament of the unification program in physics and the origin of the arrow of time are not two problems but two expressions of the same problem—information loss caused by the non-injectivity of the composite projection. Information loss has only one consequence: the coarse-grained macro description is incomplete.

**“Information Loss Rate of Macro Descriptions”** (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026c) defines the information loss rate \( D_f = H(\Omega|M') / H(\Omega) \), rigorously reduces the lower bound of inverse inference error to rate-distortion theory, gives the scaling law \( D_f \to \alpha \), and validates it with the 2D Ising model. It reports a key negative result: **\( D_f \) is time-symmetric under reversible dynamics. The non-injective projection itself does not produce the arrow of time.**

**“The Information Projection Framework”** (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026d) integrates the previous two papers into a unified framework, gives explicit convergence conditions, discusses the sensitivity of \( D_f \) to the definition of \( \Omega \), distinguishes the information projection framework from econometric identification theory, and validates the framework with real biological networks.

**“Error-Correcting Code Structure in Neural Networks”** (Zhong Shanzhen, 2026e) uses the information loss rate to quantitatively measure the information distribution in the hidden layers of neural networks. It finds: **75% of information is in the correlations among neurons**, not in single neurons, and not concentrated in a few “important” neurons. Relation has an error-correcting code structure and can tolerate partial damage without losing information. The information migration threshold is approximately \( 0.8 \times \) the number of classes. These results quantitatively correspond to the quantum error-correcting code picture in AdS/CFT duality and the island mechanism of the black hole information paradox.

Together these five papers form this paper’s scientific foundation:

- **The first** proves: inverse inference impossibility is a static structural fact.
- **The second** proves: the arrow of time and the unification predicament share the same source.
- **The third** gives a quantitative tool: information loss rate \( D_f \).
- **The fourth** integrates it into a unified framework.
- **The fifth** gives empirical proof: information is in relation.

They jointly support this paper’s core thesis: **the projection contains no information; information is in relation. Relation is immortality, because relation is the fiber folded together, with an error-correcting structure, and the fiber never vanishes.**
