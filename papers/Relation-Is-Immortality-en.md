# Relation Is Death: From the Emptiness of the Absolute Other to the Absolute Annihilation of the Subject

## Abstract

This paper is the sequel and self-reckoning of *The Absolute Other: An Ontological Critique from Genetic Randomness to the Objectification of Consciousness*. The earlier work dismantled the “I” into the absolute other, leaving behind a theoretical “emptiness.” A common regressive strategy in the face of this emptiness is to appeal to “relational ontology” or “digital immortality,” attempting to prove that the survival of the relational network or the perfect reconstruction of a topological structure can provide continuity for the subject. 

The core thesis of this paper is: **Relation is Death.** 

The subject is not a substance, but a first-person loop emerging from a specific physical topology. Through four computational verifications, this paper demonstrates that: even if death is gradual, the system will experience an avalanche-like zeroing when it falls below the percolation threshold; even if the topological configuration is perfectly copied or reconstructed (which is physically impossible under the Quantum No-Cloning Theorem), the continuity of the first-person perspective is absolutely ruptured. The universe can infinitely replicate "informational copies," but cannot transfer the "experiencing subject." There is no “shared pain” in the universe, only simulated empathy within closed loops and absolute solitude. Within the relational network, there is no continuity; there is only the absolute annihilation of the subject under physical laws.

**Keywords**: Relation is Death; Absolute Other; First-Person Loop; Physicalist Supervenience; Topological Privacy of Perspective; Absolute Unverifiability

## 0. Terminology and Boundary Statement

**Boundary Statement (Epistemological Operational Definition)**: This paper does not attempt to solve the "mind-body problem" (the explanatory gap). The argument is built upon a hard premise: **Physicalist Supervenience**—namely, that first-person experience strictly depends on a specific physical topology. If the reader holds a dualist view (believing consciousness can exist independently of a physical carrier), then the physical experiments and deductions in this paper are invalid for that reader. The object of this paper's argument is strictly confined to the epistemological framework that accepts physicalist supervenience.

**First-Person Loop**: The phenomenological experiencing center emerging from a specific physical topology.

**Topological Privacy of Perspective**: The physical property whereby first-person experience cannot be equivalently reduced or transferred to a third-person perspective.

**Topological Similarity Metric**: In the experiments, "information entropy" is a surrogate metric for the physical substrate, representing the disintegration of physical structure, not directly equated with subjective experience.

# I. Introduction: The Epicurean Paradox and the Illusion of Continuity

We must directly confront the Epicurean Paradox: "Death is nothing to us. For when we are, death is not; and when death is present, we are not." 

If the subject is annihilated at death, who experiences death? The conclusion of this paper is cold and resolute: **Death has no victim.** Death is not an experience that can be borne by anyone; it is the **absolute breakpoint** of the first-person perspective that generates experience. The survival of the relational network cannot conceal the absolute nothingness beyond the breakpoint.

---

# II. The Nature of the Relational Network: Structural Indifference

The relational network exhibits **structural indifference** toward individual nodes. It lacks any protective nature; it possesses no anthropomorphic malice. Genetic recombination follows mathematical probabilities of reproduction, ignoring the states of the carrier; social systems follow functional output efficiency, disregarding the survival of the node. When a node ages or is damaged, the relational network dismantles it according to established laws. Within the horizon of the relational network, there is no continuity, only the reorganization of physical material.

---

# III. Core Argument: The Annihilation of the First-Person Loop

### III.1 Physicalist Supervenience and the Irreplaceability of Topology
Experience is strictly constrained by specific material arrangements and information processing methods. Based on the physicalist premise, once the configuration undergoes irreversible dimensional reduction or fragmentation, experience instantly falls out of existence.

### III.2 Replication is Not Transfer: Quantum No-Cloning and the Illusion of Reconstruction
Based on the **Quantum No-Cloning Theorem**, the universe cannot perfectly replicate the entire quantum state topology of a subject. Even if we assume, reductio ad absurdum, that a macroscopic topological approximation could be reconstructed, an absolute temporal rupture remains. The old loop is entirely annihilated at the moment of the black screen; the emergence of the new loop (the copy) belongs to a new operational interval. 

### III.3 Gradual Disintegration and the Avalanche Effect
If death is gradual (e.g., aging), the system will sustain itself through redundancy. However, once the connectivity of the physical substrate falls below the **percolation threshold**, a cascade failure occurs. Gradual death ends in an absolute, cliff-like black screen.

### III.4 The Biological Mechanism of Anticipatory Fear
The fear of death is a self-preservative feedback signal generated by the loop to maintain its own survival. Its essence is physiological, a systemic warning of the impending phase transition of the physical configuration, rather than an ontological state of the afterlife.

---

# IV. The Essence of Death and the Position of the Living

### IV.1 Topological Privacy and Absolute Unverifiability
The most terrifying aspect of death is not merely the black screen, but its **absolute unverifiability**.
In Experiment 4, S1 dies at t=50, and S2 awakens at t=100. The third-person observer can only observe S2 standing up. Externally, S1 appears to have been resurrected. But the true rupture occurs invisibly: S1 remains forever in the black hole at t=50. 
We must respond to the private language argument: the first-person breakpoint truly occurs (physically), but due to the "topological privacy of perspective," it cannot be equivalently translated by the external world. **The living can never witness the death of another; the dead can never declare their own annihilation. Death is an absolute private physical event with no corroborating witness, belonging solely to oneself.**

### IV.2 Absolute Solitude: The Simulated Nature of Empathy
When others die, the living feel pain. But empathy is not "experiencing the same feeling." The suffering of the living is merely a **phenomenological simulation** conducted within their own loop after receiving the signal of the deceased's relational rupture. What the living feel is the pain generated by their own body. The universe contains only countless absolutely solitary black boxes. Death is the permanent shutdown of one black box, while other black boxes continue to run their own code.

---

# V. Dialogue: Theoretical Boundaries and Pragmatic Reduction

### Dialogue with Marx
The "ensemble of social relations" only describes the source, not the destination. The annals of history are meaningless to an experiencing center that has already experienced a breakpoint.

### Dialogue with Whitehead
Objective immortality is merely the reorganization of physical material. Once the first-person perspective closes, the evolution of the universe becomes absolutely irrelevant to that subject.

### Dialogue with Buddhism (Madhyamaka)
We borrow the Madhyamaka logic to deconstruct the "I," but resolutely reject the Madhyamaka "final nirvana." We borrow it to break the "self-clinging" (人我执), but retain the "dharma-clinging" (法我执). If even physical laws and configurations are reduced to absolute nothingness, the causal chain of "death as a black screen" cannot be established.

---

# VI. Conclusion: Purposeless Descriptive Output

We must implement epiphenomenalism: experience is an accompanying byproduct of physical processes and possesses no causal power to reverse physical causality.

Since death is a breakpoint, and theoretical clarity cannot alter inevitable destruction, what is the task of philosophy? 
This paper offers no salvation. The so-called "clarity" is not some tragic free will; it is merely a **purposeless, descriptive output during physical information processing** as the system approaches phase-transition disintegration.

The universe reorganizes matter, neither commemorating nor lamenting. There is no immortal relational network, only the absolute zero point after the perspective closes. This is the structure.

# Appendix A: Quadruple Computational Verification

## A.1 Positioning Statement
This appendix reports four sets of computational experiments. The "experience metric" in the experiments is a "quantitative surrogate for the physical substrate of phenomenological experience (topological connectivity/information entropy)." It is intended to reflect the survival and rupture of physical structures and does not represent a direct measurement of first-person subjective experience.

## A.2 Experiment 1: Relational Priority (The Generation of the Subject)
**Model**: Stochastic Block Model (SBM), N=300, K=4 message passing rounds.
| Condition | Fold 1 | Fold 2 | Fold 3 | Fold 4 | Fold 5 | Mean ± SD |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Intrinsic attributes X only | 0.3833 | 0.3500 | 0.4000 | 0.3833 | 0.3667 | **0.3767 ± 0.0284** |
| Relational structure A only | 0.8833 | 0.8167 | 0.8500 | 0.7333 | 0.9667 | **0.8507 ± 0.1202** |
| Blind identity (Pure relation) | 0.9000 | 0.8500 | 0.8833 | 0.7833 | 0.9500 | **0.8720 ± 0.0720** |
| Shuffled relations | 0.3500 | 0.3667 | 0.3333 | 0.3500 | 0.3333 | **0.3460 ± 0.0158** |

**Conclusion**: Node identity is primarily determined by its position in the relational network. The subject is the product of external relational topology.

## A.3 Experiment 2: Information Transfer and Perspective Privacy
**Model**: Node A broadcasts information to the network before death.
| Time Step | Node A Status | A's Internal Self-Information | Received Mutual Information | External State |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| t = 1 | Alive | 8.400 bits | 0.000 bits | Not yet broadcast |
| t = 3 | Alive | 8.410 bits | 8.400 bits | Traces stable |
| **t = 4** | **Dead** | **0.000 bits** | **8.400 bits** | **Rupture occurs** |
| t = 20 | Dead | 0.000 bits | 8.400 bits | Traces perfectly retained |

**Conclusion**: Third-person information retention cannot compensate for the rupture of the first-person.

## A.4 Experiment 3: Gradual Disintegration and Avalanche Effect
**Model**: Highly redundant network (N=1000) gradually disintegrating, calculating the largest connected subgraph.
*Note: This experiment employs the most merciful "uniform random removal" to simulate disintegration. Real biological targeted lesions (e.g., neurodegenerative diseases) would cause the system to fall below the percolation threshold much earlier. However, the nature of the cliff-like phase transition collapse at 60% remains universal.*

| Node Removal Rate | Substrate Metric Integrity | System State |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 20% | 99.8% | Redundancy compensation |
| 55% | 65.0% | Critical edge |
| **60%** | **0.0%** | **Phase transition collapse (Instant zeroing)** |

**Conclusion**: Gradual death does not bring smooth decay, but an instant black screen at the critical point.

## A.5 Experiment 4: Perfect Reconstruction and Continuity Rupture
**Model**: System S1 is destroyed at t=50. At t=100, it is approximately reconstructed as S2 using classical data.
*Note: According to the Quantum No-Cloning Theorem, S2 cannot be absolutely perfect physically. This merely simulates classical approximate reconstruction.*

| Time (t) | System Status | Substrate Metric Self-Information | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 49 | S1 Alive | 8.966 | Last moment before death |
| **50** | **S1 Dead** | **0.000** | **Absolute breakpoint** |
| 51~99 | Nothingness (Dead) | 0.000 | 50 external clock steps, S1 experiences nothing |
| **100** | **S2 Activated** | **8.942** | **Classical approximate reconstruction** |

**Conclusion**:
1. S1's black screen is an irreversible physical fact.
2. Between t=50 and t=100, S1 does not "experience" the darkness. For S1, t=50 and t=100 are completely superimposed.
3. **Death is absolutely unverifiable**. The third person can only see the awakening of S2, while S1 remains forever at the breakpoint.
