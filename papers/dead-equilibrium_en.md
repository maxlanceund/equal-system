## Abstract

"Teacher is asking me for a sum of money that doesn't even exist"—this nightmare posed a formalizable proposition: if everyone strips off their moral disguise and society pursues money as the sole goal, where will it go?

This paper employs multi-agent thought experiments, setting up four controlled scenarios and using controlled variable experiments to illustrate two counterintuitive findings.

**Finding One: Guarantees prevent death but cannot prevent relational death.** Controlling for all other variables, the only difference is the survival guarantee. In the unguaranteed society, after rapid population death and the cessation of exploitation, survivors' moral ceilings and trust ceilings are paradoxically *not* further eroded; in the guaranteed society, no one dies, but moral ceilings and trust ceilings are permanently lowered—cumulative damage D_social is approximately 50 times that of the unguaranteed society (mean 50.1 over 10 runs, range 48.3–52.0).

**Finding Two: A purely moral society dies from free-riding; contagion is merely an accelerant.** Across three contagion rates (0 / 0.01 / 0.05), the temporal sequence "free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later" appears in all groups; even with zero contagion, the free-riding rate rises to 77.5%, and cooperation first falls below 5%. Weak punishment delays collapse by only about 8 rounds and cannot prevent it.

This paper continues the critical tradition of Marx, Simmel, and Ostrom, but through controlled variable experiments illustrates two previously unformalized counterintuitive mechanisms. Together, these two mechanisms show that any single measure pushed to its extreme leads to the death of social relations—only the manner of death differs. This paper further proposes the **dead equilibrium index**: when cumulative damage D_social(t) is greater than or equal to 1, the system enters an irreversible dead-equilibrium zone.

## Literature Positioning

This paper engages with four strands of research. **Classical social philosophy** (Marx, Simmel) reveals the transformation of money from a means to an absolute measure, but does not formalize it into a tractable model. **Cooperation evolution and commons governance** (Axelrod, Ostrom, Putnam) discuss free-riding and institutional punishment, but presuppose that free-riders can be identified; this paper's zero-contagion experiment shows that even without contagion, as long as free-riders are not punished and still receive a share of resources, cooperation will be eroded. **Simulation as thought experiment** (Grüne-Yanoff & Weirich) argues that computer simulation can serve as a mechanized extension of thought experiments; this paper treats simulation as a conceptual operationalization tool rather than an object of epistemological analysis. **Cumulative damage** (Miner, 1945) provides the form "cumulative stress reaching a threshold causes fracture"; this paper specifies "stress" as "exploitation intensity" E(t) and renames "fracture" as "dead equilibrium."

**The boundary between "dead equilibrium" and existing concepts.** The "dead equilibrium" proposed here differs from four existing concepts: (1) **Anomie** (Durkheim) emphasizes the absence of norms, whereas this paper emphasizes the **simultaneous zeroing** of cooperation, trust, and morality; (2) **Social disintegration** emphasizes the breakdown of structure, whereas this paper emphasizes that after breakdown **ceilings are permanently punctured by history**, so revival can only return to a post-scar low point; (3) **Low-trust equilibrium** (Fukuyama) describes a state where trust is low but cooperation persists, whereas this paper describes a state where **cooperation rates go to zero**—not a low-level equilibrium, but a stillness after relational death; (4) **Institutional sclerosis** (Olson) emphasizes interest groups blocking change, whereas this paper emphasizes **irreversible cumulative damage**—not unwillingness to change, but ceilings already punctured, making return impossible. The specificity of dead equilibrium lies in: **cooperation, trust, and morality simultaneously zeroing, with ceilings permanently lowered**.

In sum, this paper's academic position is: at the intersection of cooperation evolution and social philosophy, using multi-agent thought experiments to deduce the logical limit of "money as the sole measure," proposing the concept of "dead equilibrium" and the formula of the "dead equilibrium index."

## Theoretical Framework and Methodological Statement

### Core Concepts

**Dead equilibrium (class concept)**: The general state of social-relational death—cooperation rate zero, trust zero, morality zero, social relations entering a state of low-level repetition or stillness. It has two concrete forms:

- **Form A: Demographic-collapse dead equilibrium** (unguaranteed scenario): After mass population death, exploitation targets disappear, exploitation stops, and survivors fall into isolation.
- **Form B: Scarred zombie equilibrium** (guaranteed scenario): People do not die, but cooperation, trust, and morality all go to zero; exploitation continues but no longer produces any repair; moral ceilings and trust ceilings are permanently lowered.

**Scarred zombie equilibrium**: Form B of dead equilibrium. People do not die, but cooperation, trust, and morality all go to zero; debt-sense and trauma continue to accumulate; moral ceilings and trust ceilings are permanently lowered; exploitation continues but no longer produces repair.

**Scarring effect**: The ceilings of trust and morality are permanently lowered by historical exploitation; subsequent cooperation can only repair up to the ceiling, not back to the original level.

**Debt ontology**: Not an economic theory of debt, but an existential situation—the sense of debt precedes any actual contract. It approaches the "recognition" problem in the Hegel–Kojève tradition: the depth of the sense of debt is not about owing money, but about not being recognized as an end in oneself. The debt-sense in the model is an operational approximation of it, not the whole.

**Demographic death vs. social-relational death**: Demographic death refers to mass death of agents (e.g., 76%) or total death; social-relational death refers to cooperation, trust, and morality going to zero. This paper uses **cooperation rate first falling below 5%** as the operational indicator of social-relational death—this does not mean all social ties disappear, but that **voluntary, non-coercive cooperative relations** disappear. In the model setup, cooperation is the only non-exploitative form of interaction; therefore, cooperation going to zero means social relations are reduced to exploitation or isolation.

### Methodological Statement

**Simulation is a mechanized extension of thought experiments; its conclusions are conditional, not predictive.** The role of simulation is not empirical induction but mechanism deduction: decomposing "what if everyone only recognizes money" into trackable rules and feedback, exposing emergent outcomes that pure speculation easily misses. The boundary of conclusions is conditional: not "reality must be so," but "if mechanism M holds, logical limit L may emerge." The role of controlled variable experiments is to rule out alternative explanations: if two scenarios are completely identical except for one variable, then the difference in results can only be attributed to that variable. Robustness checks (Appendix A.11) further confirm that conclusions do not depend on a single run, specific settings, or specific parameters.

### Response to "The Conclusion Is Not New"

This paper does not deny the oldness of the conclusion. Marx, Simmel, and Ostrom have already revealed the corrosion of social relations by money. The increments of this paper, in order of priority: **First**, the counterintuitive conclusion of Finding One—guarantees prevent death but cannot prevent relational death, with a D_social difference of about 50 times; **Second**, the symmetry of Finding Two—the failure of two utopias, monetary society dying from trust zeroing, moral society dying from free-riding; **Third**, the formalization of the dead equilibrium index.

**The conditionality of the two findings must be stated clearly.** Finding One holds under the condition that guarantees provide only a survival floor and no trust-repair mechanism; R2 tested only a mild positive mechanism (trust recovery +20%, moral decay −30%, cooperation base probability +0.05), and D_social only dropped from 1362 to 1355, so stronger interventions cannot be ruled out. Finding Two holds under the condition that free-riding is unpunished or only weakly punished; R3 tested only weak punishment (10% detection probability), which delayed collapse by only about 8 rounds, so stronger punishment cannot be ruled out. **Both findings are "conditional conclusions under specific mechanism settings," not universal assertions that "guarantees are necessarily ineffective" or "punishment is necessarily ineffective."**

### Scope of Validity

The conclusions of this paper apply only to the deduction of the specific logic "money as the sole measure," and are not applicable to predicting reality or analyzing specific historical societies. R2 tested only mild positive mechanisms; whether stronger interventions (e.g., external institutional punishment, forced redistribution) are effective is beyond the scope of this paper.

## I. Introduction: From a Nightmare to a Proposition

I dreamed that my high school teacher was asking me for a sum of money that didn't even exist. I didn't have this money, but the sense of judgment was so real: everyone wore the face of "you owe me," morality was reduced to decoration, and money measured everything. After waking, a question lingered:

> **If everyone strips off their moral disguise and society pursues money as the sole goal, with money measuring everything, where will society go?**

This is not an empirical sociological question, but a formalizable philosophical proposition. This paper does not predict reality, but uses multi-agent thought experiments to deduce the limit of this logic: under what conditions it collapses, under what conditions it forms a steady state, and whether this steady state is reversible. In the following, "this paper" refers to the author's analytical position; the "I" in the introduction serves only to introduce the empirical starting point of the question.

## II. Theoretical Framework: Monetary Alienation, Debt Ontology, and Will to Power

### 2.1 Money from Means to Absolute Measure

Simmel pointed out that money is originally a "means"; when it becomes an "absolute means," people forget it is only a means. Marx further revealed: money conceals social relations, turning relations between people into relations between things. When money becomes the sole measure, it is no longer merely a medium of exchange, but becomes the measure of success, dignity, relationships, morality, and even "whether a person is worth being treated as a person."

### 2.2 Debt Ontology: The Ontologization of "You Owe Me"

The most insidious mechanism of monetary society is to fold all relations into an account book: favors, feelings, recognition, security—all calculated as "accounts payable." More extreme: **debt does not need to actually exist**—as long as the feeling of "you owe me" is manufactured, exploitation is established. The "nonexistent money" in the nightmare is precisely the concretization of this debt ontology.

The "debt ontology" in this paper is not an economic theory of debt, but an existential situation—the sense of debt precedes actual contracts. It approaches the "recognition" problem in the Hegel–Kojève tradition (Hegel, 1807; Kojève, 1947): the depth of the sense of debt is not about owing money, but about not being recognized as an end in oneself. The debt-sense in the model is an operational approximation of it, not the whole.

### 2.3 Will to Power and Moral Decoration

Nietzsche's will to power is often misread as "stepping on others to climb up." More accurately, it is the impulse of life to expand and transcend itself. When the will to power is guided only by money, it no longer creates, only plunders; when morality becomes decoration, it is no longer "why I do this," but "whom I can accuse with this." The sense of judgment in the nightmare is precisely the product of the will to power putting on the cloak of money and picking up moral decoration. The model in this paper simulates only the **shadow side** of the will to power (predatory expansion), not its creative side. The "exploitation tendency" in the model is only its degraded form under the monetary measure.

## III. Model Design

### 3.1 Agents and Network

200 agents, ring network, each agent has 4 neighbors. Each agent possesses: wealth, morality, moral ceiling, trust, trust ceiling, debt-sense, trauma memory, free-rider status. Initial morality is 0.35–0.65 (the pure_moral scenario is separately set to 0.30–0.80).

### 3.2 Core Interaction

Each round, neighboring pairs are matched; cooperation probability depends simultaneously on morality, trust, debt-sense, and trauma. If cooperation does not occur, whether exploitation occurs is determined by the moral gap and wealth difference. Exploitation can only go from rich to poor, and the transfer amount has an absolute upper limit. The exploited party's debt-sense and trauma accumulate; trust ceiling and moral ceiling are permanently lowered. Successful cooperation repairs morality and trust, but repair cannot exceed the ceiling.

### 3.3 Time Irreversibility: Scarring Effect

This setting has a real-world foundation: trust is built gradually, destroyed instantaneously, and the effort required for reconstruction far exceeds initial construction; certain moral injuries—such as being treated as a tool, being denied purposiveness—cannot be offset by subsequent good deeds. This is not a matter of quantity but of quality (what Margalit calls "moral injury").

### 3.4 Four Controlled Scenarios

**Table 1 Scenario Settings**

## V. Controlled Variable Experiments: Two Counterintuitive Findings

### 5.1 Finding One (Experiment E): Guarantees Prevent Death but Cannot Prevent Relational Death

**Design**: Control moral decay, punishment, exploitation intensity, and cooperation base probability to be completely identical; the only difference is the survival guarantee (0 vs. 15).

**Table 6 Guaranteed vs. Unguaranteed (Round 50 Comparison, Social-Relational Indicators Only)**

| Indicator | Unguaranteed (Round 50) | Guaranteed (Round 50) | Comparison of Social-Relational Indicators |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cooperation rate | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | Equal |
| Moral ceiling | 0.9986 | 0.7830 | Guaranteed lower (worse) |
| Trust ceiling | 0.9972 | 0.5447 | Guaranteed lower (worse) |
| Debt-sense | 0.3995 | 32.0593 | Guaranteed higher (worse) |

Note: This table compares only social-relational indicators, not overall welfare.

**Table 7 Trajectory of Social Cumulative Damage D_social (seed=42)**

| Round | Unguaranteed D_social | Guaranteed D_social |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 1.90 | 1.90 |
| 25 | 26.81 | 69.82 |
| 50 | 27.78 | 173.64 |
| 100 | 27.80 | 411.25 |
| 200 | 27.80 | 886.13 |
| 300 | 27.80 | 1362.20 |

**Robustness and Alternative Model**: Across 10 independent runs (seed=1–10), the mean multiple is 50.11 (SD 1.18, range 48.34–52.01), so the core conclusion does not depend on a single run. After adding a mild positive mechanism (trust recovery +20%, moral decay −30%, cooperation base probability +0.05), the round-300 cooperation rate remains 0.0000, and D_social is 1355.09—the conclusion is not an artifact of "guarantees having only the function of preventing death."

**Significance**: After mass population death, exploitation targets disappear, survivor networks fragment, exploitation stops, and damage accumulation tends to flatten; guarantees keep people alive but allow damage to continue accumulating. Guarantees change the form of death but not its direction. From the perspective of demographic survival, guarantees raise the survival rate; but the finding of this paper is that a higher survival rate does not equal the maintenance of social relations—guarantees alone are insufficient to solve the problem of social-relational death.

### 5.2 Finding Two (Experiment F): A Purely Moral Society Dies from Free-riding; Contagion Is Merely an Accelerant

**Design**: Control moral level, cooperation base probability, and public-pool mode to be completely identical; the only difference is the free-riding contagion rate (0 / 0.01 / 0.05).

**Table 8 Effect of Free-riding Contagion Rate on Cooperation Collapse**

| Contagion Rate | Round Free-riding Reaches 30% | Round Cooperation First Falls Below 5% | Temporal Relation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Zero contagion | 81 | 200 | Free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later |
| Low contagion (0.01) | 39 | 93 | Free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later |
| High contagion (0.05) | 22 | 52 | Free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later |

Note: All three groups show the temporal sequence "free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later." Even with zero contagion, the free-riding rate rises to 77.5%, and cooperation first falls below 5%. Weak punishment (10% detection probability, deduct 10% wealth, trust −0.05) delays the round of cooperation first falling below 5% from 200 to 208, and the final cooperation rate rises from 1.75% to 4.84%—delayed by about 8 rounds, but still below the 5% collapse threshold.

**Mechanism Explanation**: In the pure_moral scenario, free-riders appear randomly with a very small probability (0.5%/round), unaffected by moral level; once they appear, free-riders do not cooperate but still receive a share of the pool, and cooperators, finding their returns diluted, reduce their willingness to cooperate. **A high moral level can only reduce the probability of cooperators actively exploiting; it cannot prevent free-riders from randomly appearing and cooperators from exiting due to diluted returns**—this is the mechanism behind "morality not decayed but cooperation still collapses."

**Significance**: The purely moral society does not die from "contagion," but from the mechanism "free-riders do not cooperate but still receive a share of the pool." The difference from Ostrom (1990): Ostrom presupposes that free-riders can be identified and punished; this paper's zero-contagion experiment shows that even without contagion, as long as free-riders are not punished and still receive resources, cooperation will be eroded. The weak-punishment test further shows that punishment intensity is a key variable—weak punishment is insufficient; stronger or more systematic punishment mechanisms are needed.

## VI. Dead Equilibrium Index: A Cumulative Damage Model

### 6.1 From Simulation to Formalization

Qualitative conclusions can only tell the reader "it will die," not "how dead counts as dead" or "when is it still not too late." This section writes the three structures repeatedly appearing in the simulation—conditionality of cooperation, historical dependence of ceilings, and cumulativeness of collapse—into readable equations. These equations are induced from the simulation, not derived a priori; their legitimacy comes from the simulation, not from axioms.

### 6.2 Micro: Conditional Equation of Cooperation

$$
P_{coop}(i,j) = c_0 \cdot \frac{m_i+m_j}{2} \cdot \frac{t_i+t_j}{2} \cdot \max\left(0, 1-\frac{d_i+d_j}{D_{max}}\right) \cdot \max\left(0, 1-\frac{\tau_i+\tau_j}{\tau_{max}}\right)
$$

(Equation 1: Conditional Equation of Cooperation)

In the simulation, D_max = 60, tau_max = 200. Cooperation is not a single-variable function of morality, but the product of morality, trust, debt, and trauma. If any term goes to zero, the cooperation probability goes to zero.

### 6.3 Macro: Three Core Dynamic Equations

$$
\frac{dM}{dt} = r_m C - \delta_m M - \kappa (1-M) - \eta P
$$

(Equation 2: Moral Dynamics)

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} = r_t C - \beta E - \gamma T
$$

(Equation 3: Trust Dynamics)

$$
C(t) \approx c_0 \cdot M(t) \cdot T(t) \cdot \left(1-\frac{D(t)}{D_{max}}\right) \cdot \left(1-\frac{S(t)}{S_{max}}\right)
$$

(Equation 4: Cooperation Rate)

Equations (2)–(4) describe reversible dynamics: if C rebounds, M and T can be repaired. But the simulation shows that repair has a ceiling, and the ceiling itself is punctured by history. The coefficients are fixed constants in the simulation; specific values see Appendix A code.

### 6.4 Time Irreversibility: Damage Equation of Ceilings

$$
\frac{dT_{ceil}}{dt} = -\alpha E(t), \qquad \frac{dM_{ceil}}{dt} = -\alpha' E(t)
$$

(Equation 5: Rate of Ceiling Damage)

$$
T_{ceil}(t) = T_{ceil}(0) - \alpha \int_0^t E(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

(Equation 6: Integral of Trust Ceiling)

$$
M_{ceil}(t) = M_{ceil}(0) - \alpha' \int_0^t E(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

(Equation 7: Integral of Moral Ceiling)

The ceiling is not a function of the current state, but an integral of the historical path. Once exploitation occurs, the integral increases, and the ceiling is permanently lowered; it cannot be "made up" by subsequent cooperation.

### 6.5 Dead Equilibrium Index: Cumulative Damage Integral

$$
D_{social}(t) = \int_0^t \frac{E(\tau)}{E_{crit}}\,d\tau
$$

(Equation 8: Social Cumulative Damage)

$$
D_{social}(t) \geq 1 \quad \text{indicates the system has entered an irreversible dead-equilibrium zone}
$$

(Equation 9: Dead Equilibrium Criterion)

In the simulation, E_crit = 100. The index has three meanings: D < 1 the system is still in the reversible zone; D = 1 critical point; D > 1 dead equilibrium, revival can only return to the post-scar low point.

The data of Finding One illustrate the discriminating power of this index: with seed=42, the unguaranteed D_social stabilizes at 27.80, while the guaranteed one rises to 1362.20 (about 49 times; 10-run mean 50.1 times). The E_crit sensitivity test (50/100/200) shows that round-300 D_social is 2724.40/1362.20/681.10 respectively, and the rounds to reach 1 are 1, 1, and 2 respectively—exploitation density is high in the early model, so cumulative damage exceeds the threshold early. This indicates that the index is more suitable for **comparing relative damage across scenarios** than for judging the absolute collapse point of a single scenario. The absolute value of the index depends on E_crit calibration; its use is structural diagnosis, not precise prediction of "which round collapses."

### 6.6 Boundary Conditions

1. **Induction rather than deduction**: Equations (1)–(9) are induced from the simulation, not derived from axioms.
2. **Approximation rather than precision**: The simulation has randomness, soft ceilings, and discrete rounds; the equations are continuous and deterministic simplifications.
3. **Conditional conclusions**: The equations do not predict reality; they describe only the logical relations of mechanisms.

The critical point in reality can only be indirectly estimated through institutional indicators: E(t) can be approximated by the annual change in debt-dispute rates or the "distrust" proportion in trust surveys; E_crit can be calibrated by the cumulative dispute rate before social trust collapse in historical data. Specific operations require empirical research.

## VII. Discussion: Philosophical Implications of Dead Equilibrium

### 7.1 Why Dead Equilibrium Is Bad: A Normative Argument

This paper adopts a hybrid position of deontology and perfectionism. **From deontology**: dead equilibrium is bad not because it makes people poor (consequence), but because it reduces people to prices and cancels the non-tradable; when all relations are folded into accounts, people are no longer ends in themselves, but payable items on others' ledgers (Kant, 1785). **From perfectionism**: cooperation, trust, and friendship are constitutive elements of a good life; dead equilibrium cancels them and thereby blocks human flourishing. The two frameworks agree on the issue of dead equilibrium: it simultaneously violates "persons as ends" and blocks flourishing.

### 7.2 Time Irreversibility: What Is Rebuilt Is Not the Original

The scarring effect shows: moral ceiling permanently drops from 0.998 to 0.302, trust ceiling from 1.00 to 0.30. Even if "moral revival" occurs, it can only return to the post-scar low point. This explains why moral reconstruction in history is often accompanied by institutional reconstruction—human warmth alone cannot repair ceilings that have been permanently lowered. What is rebuilt is not the original thing, but morality and trust with scars. This is consistent with Putnam's (2000) observation on the loss of social capital.

### 7.3 Morality Is Not a Panacea, and Money Is Not the Only Evil

A purely moral society dies from free-riding; a purely monetary society dies from trust zeroing. The problem is not "money or morality," but "whether there are mechanisms to protect cooperation, punish free-riding, and maintain the balance between incentives and trust." Any single measure pushed to its extreme will die; only the manner of death differs. Guarantees can prevent death but cannot prevent relational death; morality can prevent evil but cannot prevent free-riding; weak punishment is insufficient; stronger or more systematic punishment mechanisms are needed.

## VIII. Conclusion: Not a Prophecy, but an Alarm

1. **The most terrifying aspect of a monetary society is not sudden death, but the "scarred zombie equilibrium" (Form B of dead equilibrium)**: people do not die, cooperation, trust, and morality go to zero, exploitation continues but no longer produces repair, and debt-sense continues to accumulate.
2. **Time is irreversible**: once moral ceilings and trust ceilings are punctured, revival can only return to the post-scar low point, not to the original level.
3. **Guarantees prevent death but cannot prevent relational death**: D_social is about 50 times that of the unguaranteed society (10-run mean 50.1, range 48.3–52.0). Guarantees raise the survival rate but do not maintain social relations.
4. **A purely moral society dies from free-riding; contagion is merely an accelerant**: even with zero contagion, the free-riding rate rises to 77.5%, and cooperation first falls below 5%. Weak punishment delays collapse by only about 8 rounds; punishment intensity is the key variable.
5. **Dead equilibrium index**: when D_social(t) reaches 1, the system enters an irreversible dead-equilibrium zone. The index is more suitable for comparing relative damage across scenarios than for judging the absolute collapse point of a single scenario; its absolute value depends on E_crit calibration.
6. **Way out**: establish institutional and ethical boundaries that protect cooperation, punish free-riding, and guard the non-tradable; rebuild trust before scars accumulate irreversibly.

> *"Money can be a necessary condition, but not the sole measure. Dead equilibrium is not inevitable, but it is a logical limit. Those who see the dead equilibrium have a chance not to become part of it."*

---

## IX. Falsifiability Conditions

1. **If the structural model shows that a guaranteed monetary society can maintain a cooperation rate above 20% and that moral ceilings and trust ceilings are not permanently punctured**—this would negate the inevitability of the "scarred zombie equilibrium."
2. **If the structural model shows that a purely moral society can maintain cooperation without collapse in the presence of free-riding**—this would negate the conclusion of "utopian atrophy."
3. **If the structural model shows that an unguaranteed monetary society can restore cooperation without death**—this would negate the conclusion of "rapid collapse."
4. **If empirical research shows that the ceilings of trust and morality are fully reversible**—this would negate the mechanism of "time irreversibility."

Conditions 1–3 have been tested in the model of this paper, and the results support the conclusions; Condition 4 is an empirical question; the model provides only conditional deduction and cannot replace empirical research.

## Appendix A: Structural Model Verification

### A.1 Model Settings and Main Parameters

200 agents, ring network, 4 neighbors each. Each round, neighboring pairs are matched; cooperation probability depends on morality, trust, debt-sense, and trauma. Exploitation can only go from rich to poor, and transfer amounts have an absolute upper limit. The exploited party's debt-sense and trauma accumulate; trust ceiling and moral ceiling are permanently lowered. Successful cooperation repairs morality and trust, but not beyond the ceiling.

**Table A1 Main Parameters**

| Parameter | Value | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| N | 200 | Number of agents |
| ROUNDS | 300 | Simulation rounds |
| D_max | 60 | Debt-sense suppression threshold |
| tau_max | 200 | Trauma suppression threshold |
| E_crit | 100 | Critical exploitation intensity |
| consume | 10 (monetary) / 12 (pure moral) | Survival cost per round |
| scar_factor | 0.005 | Scarring coefficient (monetary scenarios) |
| exploit_gain | 0.55 | Base exploitation probability (monetary scenarios) |

Note: The coefficients of Equations (2)–(4) are fixed constants in the code; specific values see Appendix A code.

### A.2 Experiment A: Healthy Society

**Hypothesis**: When moral decay is very low, punishment is strong, there is no guarantee and no free-riding, society can stabilize.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. Cooperation rate 50%, zero death, morality 0.996, trust 0.99. Data see main text Table 2.

### A.3 Experiment B: Unguaranteed Monetary Society

**Hypothesis**: With moderate morality + money orientation, weak punishment, and no guarantee, society collapses rapidly.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. 69% death by round 25, 76% death by round 50, cooperation goes to zero. Data see main text 4.2.

### A.4 Experiment C: Guaranteed Monetary Society

**Hypothesis**: With moderate morality + money orientation, weak punishment, and guarantee, society does not die but enters the scarred zombie equilibrium.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. Cooperation goes to zero, moral ceiling 0.302, trust ceiling 0.30, D_social 1362.20. Data see main text Table 3.

### A.5 Experiment D: Pure Morality Without Money

**Hypothesis**: When everyone is moral, there is no money, no punishment for free-riding, and a public pool exists, society may atrophy due to free-riding contagion.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. In the high-contagion group, free-riding exceeds 30% at round 22, cooperation first falls below 5% at round 52, and total extinction eventually occurs. Data see main text Table 4.

### A.6 Experiment E: Guaranteed vs. Unguaranteed (Controlled Variable)

**Hypothesis**: Controlling for other variables, the guaranteed group's D_social is significantly higher than the unguaranteed group, and moral ceiling and trust ceiling are lower than the unguaranteed group.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. Round 50 moral ceiling 0.7830 vs. 0.9986, trust ceiling 0.5447 vs. 0.9972, D_social about 50 times. Data see main text Tables 6 and 7.

### A.7 Experiment F: Purely Moral Society, Free-riding Contagion Rate (Controlled Variable)

**Hypothesis**: Free-riding rises first, cooperation collapses later; even with zero contagion, free-riding erodes cooperation.
**Conclusion**: Supports hypothesis. The temporal sequence is consistent across three groups; the zero-contagion group also collapses. Data see main text Table 8.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A | Healthy society can stabilize | Supported (cooperation rate 50%, zero death) |
| B | Unguaranteed monetary society collapses rapidly | Supported (76% death) |

**All six tests support the core structural claims of the paper.**

### A.9 Experimental Boundaries

The experiment tests "the evolutionary limit of a social system under given mechanisms" and does not involve any specific real society. The experiment cannot test "whether real society will move toward dead equilibrium" (an empirical question), cannot test specific historical cases (historical research), and cannot test love, friendship, faith, art, and other non-tradable goods (not fully modeled, yet they are precisely the "hope" in reality).

### A.11.2 R2: Alternative Model Test

| Scenario | Cooperation Rate (300) | Moral Ceiling (300) | Trust Ceiling (300) | D_social (300) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Guarantee only prevents death | 0.0000 | 0.2971 | 0.2913 | 1362.20 |
| Guarantee + positive mechanism | 0.0000 | 0.2973 | 0.2904 | 1355.09 |

**Conclusion**: After adding a mild positive mechanism, the cooperation rate still goes to zero, and D_social only drops from 1362 to 1355 (0.5% difference). This shows that a positive mechanism of this magnitude is insufficient to reverse the trend. But this paper tested only a mild mechanism—the mechanism strength is limited (trust recovery +20%, moral decay −30%, cooperation base probability +0.05), and it cannot be claimed that "any positive mechanism is ineffective." Whether stronger interventions are effective see the Scope of Validity statement.

### A.11.3 R3: Weak Punishment Test

| Scenario | Round Free-riding Reaches 30% | Round Cooperation First Falls Below 5% | Final Cooperation Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No punishment (zero contagion) | 81 | 200 | 0.0175 |
| Weak punishment (zero contagion) | 78 | 208 | 0.0484 |

**Conclusion**: Weak punishment delays collapse by about 8 rounds; the final cooperation rate rises from 1.75% to 4.84%, still below the 5% collapse threshold. Punishment intensity is the key variable—weak punishment is insufficient; stronger or more systematic punishment mechanisms are needed.

### A.11.4 R4: E_crit Sensitivity Analysis

| E_crit | D_social (300) | Round to Reach 1 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 50 | 2724.40 | 1 |
| 100 | 1362.20 | 1 |
| 200 | 681.10 | 2 |

**Conclusion**: The absolute value of D_social depends on E_crit, but the "round to reach 1" is 1, 1, and 2 respectively. Exploitation density is high in the early model, and cumulative damage exceeds the threshold early—this indicates that the index is more suitable for comparing relative damage across scenarios than for judging the absolute collapse point of a single scenario. Empirical calibration of E_crit is future work.

### A.11.5 Comprehensive Conclusion of Robustness Checks

| Check | Thesis Tested | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| R1 | Does the conclusion depend on a single run? | No; the multiple is stable between 48.3 and 52.0 (10 runs) |
| R2 | Is the conclusion an artifact of "guarantees having only the function of preventing death"? | No; after adding a mild positive mechanism, cooperation still goes to zero; but mechanism strength is limited, so stronger interventions cannot be ruled out |
| R3 | Can weak punishment prevent free-riding collapse? | No; it only delays by about 8 rounds, still below the 5% threshold |
| R4 | Does the index depend on a specific value of E_crit? | The absolute value depends, but the round to reach 1 is stable; the index is suitable for comparing relative damage, not for judging the absolute collapse point |

None of the four checks overturns any core conclusion, but two boundaries are clarified: R2 tested only a mild positive mechanism; whether stronger interventions are effective is future work. R4 shows that the absolute value of the dead equilibrium index depends on E_crit calibration; the index is for comparing relative damage across scenarios, not for judging the absolute collapse point of a single scenario. **All four checks jointly support: the core findings of the paper are robust, and their boundaries are honest.**

**Finding Two did not undergo multi-seed robustness testing**: the three contagion rates of pure_moral are currently single-run (seed=42). This is a limitation of this paper; multi-seed testing is future work.
