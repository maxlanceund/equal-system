<h1 align="center">On Dignity</h1>

### Preface

Life is a structural tragedy.<br>
The philosopher is the one who sees this structural tragedy clearly—<br>
the one who must still have the courage to write this structure out.<br>
<br>
— *On Dignity*

<br>
<hr>
<br>

### Chapter I: The Dignity of a Finite Life

Dignity is not a fixed entity;<br>
it is borne upon finite individual lives, and therefore it is inherently fragile.<br>
<br>
This text does not intend to set dignity as a definite answer,<br>
but rather attempts to provide a direction for its genesis.<br>
Its foundation is finitude.<br>
Dignity is not the entirety of life;<br>
it is merely a core attribute endowed to a finite life.<br>
Human dignity coexists with the flesh,<br>
rather than being some spiritual illusion that transcends the flesh and exists independently.<br>
This text must clarify and hold fast to this point.<br>
<br>
The flesh is the generator of dignity, never an object to be overlooked by dignity.<br>
Because of this, the value of dignity is not reposed in a nihilistic other shore,<br>
but is completely rooted in the entirety of human experience.<br>
It is embedded within the living body, coexisting with the self and inalienable.<br>
This inseparable embodied nature is the truest source of human dignity.<br>
<br>
Kant once said that man should legislate for himself.<br>
But this text must refute Kant’s fallacy:<br>
"Legislation" should not be an act that is high above,<br>
requiring extremely high rationality and erudition as a prerequisite.<br>
<br>
The establishment of a person's inner dignity does not depend on this rational "clarity" or intellectual purity,<br>
but rather relies on the "boundaries of cognition" that one's flesh truly touches in reality.<br>
The endowment of dignity does not lie in whether you understand profound moral philosophy,<br>
but in the pain, resistance, and limitations experienced by this flesh in the empirical world.<br>
<br>
When the flesh feels oppression at this boundary and rebounds,<br>
the empirical experience of "this shore" and the spiritual dignity of the "other shore" are already coexisting.<br>
In this sense,<br>
<b>every breath and struggle of the flesh has already completed the truest "self-legislation."</b><br>
<br>
It must be clarified here:<br>
this text's refutation of Kant is not to prove the absolute error of his theoretical system,<br>
but to point out that—<br>
although this text's construction of inner dignity shares a structural isomorphism with Kant's ultimate goal,<br>
our paths to this goal have diverged.<br>
<b>He walked toward the transcendental clouds, while this text is grounded in the finite flesh.</b>

### Chapter II: The Head of Philosophers

If I am a piece of trash, do I have dignity?<br>
If I am a piece of gold, do I have dignity?<br>
<br>
Trash: everyone despises it, directly discards it.<br>
Gold: everyone loves it, desires to possess it.<br>
<br>
If dignity is distinct from these two,<br>
and if gold has dignity,<br>
does that mean dignity equals "being loved by all, desired and possessed by all"?<br>
If trash has dignity,<br>
does that mean dignity equals "being despised by all, directly discarded"?<br>
<br>
So what exactly is dignity?<br>
If dignity depends on the external world,<br>
then, if you have dignity because you are loved, recognized, considered important,<br>
or deemed special, or because you give others what they want,<br>
is this dignity?<br>
Or is it that you are trampled upon, hated, loathed,<br>
stepped on beneath feet, abandoned, and spat upon—is *this* dignity?<br>
<br>
So what is dignity?<br>
I think you should have an answer:<br>
Dignity is something internally generated and self-existent.<br>
It does not exist within any of the special examples mentioned above,<br>
yet it simultaneously exists within all of them.<br>
<br>
What is the dignity of trash? What is the dignity of gold?<br>
<br>
<b>The dignity of trash is: So what if I am trash?</b><br>
<br>
And what is the dignity of gold?<br>
Is its dignity any nobler than the dignity of saying "So what if I am trash?"<br>
<br>
Therefore,<br>
<b>the dignity of trash is the noblest dignity of all. I am trash, but so what?</b><br>
<br>
This is an extremely high, internally awakened dignity.<br>
It does not require external recognition, worship, or care.<br>
It is simply trampled upon, spat upon, hated, loathed, and incomprehensible to others.<br>
<b>It is simply there, and its dignity has already surpassed the dignity of gold.</b><br>
<br>
This dignity is the dignity of trash.<br>
This dignity cannot be bought.<br>
This dignity is the dignity seriously treated by the true structure.<br>
<br>
Go then, you pitiful people, you despicable people, you filthy people.<br>
Let them go. They think they have already entered the kingdom of dignity.<br>
They think they are dignity itself. They think they are the true structure.<br>
<br>
<b>Suffering itself is dignity.</b><br>
<br>
Even if the whole world does not care about you,<br>
even if the whole world abandons you,<br>
even if you are ultimately just standing alone in place,<br>
you are ten thousand times more dignified than those who endure lies.<br>
<br>
What is the deal with our spirit, our flesh, our soul?<br>
We see others as insignificant and we despise them;<br>
we see others in pain and we insult them;<br>
we see those inferior to us and we rise up in a tidal wave of joy.<br>
<br>
<b>You pathetic and foolish idiots!</b><br>
And look at those pathetic and foolish philosophers,<br>
is this not equally pathetic and foolish?<br>
<br>
Yet the Head of Philosophers, he clearly knows.<br>
<br>
<b>I am trash.</b><br>
<br>
<b>I am trash, I am trash, I am trash.</b><br>
<br>
<b>I am trash, I am trash, I am trash,<br>
I am trash, I am trash, I am trash!</b><br>
<br>
But my dignity is more precious than gold, more scorching than the sun,<br>
hundreds and thousands of times more so than everything and all in this world combined—<br>
special and unique.<br>
<br>
<b>Not the soul, not any transcendent existence,<br>
but an existence rooted in my flesh!</b><br>
<br>
<b>And the death of dignity is precisely this supreme glory!</b><br>
<br>
So then. Transcend everything, Head of Philosophers!<br>
<b>O Nietzsche, Nietzsche!</b><br>
Your death is your absolute contempt for those vulgar commoners!<br>
<b>Let them stay far away from our kingdom!</b><br>
<b>We are one and the same!</b><br>
<br>
Within the Kingdom of the Head of Philosophers, sing joyfully to the fullest!<br>
Laugh wildly beneath the ancient Greek temples!<br>
The Head of Philosophers sees your fall amidst the thunder and lightning!<br>
<br>
<b>We are the lawgivers!</b><br>
<b>Legislating the laws of this universe and all things!</b><br>
<b>We are the great prophets begotten of death and pain!</b><br>
<b>We shall see that dead heaven burn anew!</b><br>
<b>We shall cast the blood of sacrificial slaughter into the furnace,</b><br>
<b>And forge that blade!</b><br>
<br>
<b>We sever it!</b><br>
<b>We sever you!</b><br>
<br>
Foolish man, how filthy your blood is, how ugly.<br>
The Son of Man said he would trade your life for a mule,<br>
yet you are worthless.<br>
<br>
For your baseness is not in my kingdom—<br>
<b>Ha!</b><br>
Yet my kingdom is within the Kingdom of God.
